Demixed Principal Component Analysis

Demixed Principal Component Analysis

Problem

Demixed principal component analysis (dPCA) constructs supervised linear coordinates that approximately reconstruct declared task-variable marginalizations of multivariate data. It was developed for trial-structured neural population recordings in which activity varies with time and experimentally labeled conditions, while individual variables and ordinary principal components often mix several task dependencies (Brendel et al. 2011; Kobak et al. 2016).

The word demixed names success under a particular label decomposition and reconstruction loss. It does not mean that the resulting components are statistically independent, causally separated, uniquely identified, anatomically localized, or generated by distinct neural modules. The method is a label-dependent linear summary, not blind source separation or a mechanistic model. The bounded formulation here follows the published 2016 analysis of trial-averaged condition means; it is not a trial-level noise-covariance model (Kobak et al. 2016).

Input

Let \(N\) be the number of measured features or neurons, \(T\) the number of aligned time bins, and let \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\) denote declared categorical or discretized task variables. For a balanced full-factorial design, a trial-resolved tensor can be written

\[ \mathcal X\in \mathbb R^{N\times T\times A_1\times\cdots\times A_m\times K}, \]

where \(K\) is the same number of trials in every condition cell. The fitted input in the bounded formulation here is the trial-averaged condition tensor

\[ \bar{\mathcal X} \in \mathbb R^{N\times T\times A_1\times\cdots\times A_m}, \]

flattened into a centered matrix \(X\in\mathbb R^{N\times M}\), with \(M=T\prod_j A_j\). Each condition–time column is given equal weight in the bounded formulation below. The trial-resolved tensor supplies independent repeats for estimating these means and for cross-validation; its individual trial columns do not replace \(X\) in the objective stated below.

A reproducible analysis must declare:

  • the experimental unit, trial inclusion rules and whether features were recorded simultaneously or assembled as a pseudo-population;
  • alignment, binning, smoothing, time warping and baseline subtraction;
  • feature scaling and centering;
  • the task variables, their coding, level order and which interactions are retained or grouped;
  • whether all factorial cells exist and how unequal trial counts are handled;
  • the ranks \(q_\phi\) allocated to each marginalization;
  • the regularization grid and cross-validation split unit; and
  • whether projections will be applied to condition means, single trials, or both.

Equal weighting of condition means answers a different question from weighting cells in proportion to their trial counts. Missing cells, correlated labels and unequal noise precision require an explicit model, weighting or rebalancing procedure rather than silent application of balanced formulas (Kobak et al. 2016).

Output

For each declared marginalization group \(\phi\), dPCA returns:

  • a target matrix \(X_\phi\) with the same shape as \(X\);
  • a decoder matrix \(D_\phi\in\mathbb R^{q_\phi\times N}\) whose rows define linear projection axes;
  • an encoder matrix \(F_\phi\in\mathbb R^{N\times q_\phi}\) used to reconstruct the corresponding marginalization;
  • component scores \(Z_\phi=D_\phi X\); and
  • an approximate marginal reconstruction \(\widehat X_\phi=F_\phi D_\phi X\).

The combined approximation is

\[ \widehat X=\sum_\phi F_\phi D_\phi X. \]

Components belonging to different marginalizations are not generally orthogonal, and their scores need not be uncorrelated. The output is deterministic given the fitted data and choices; it is not a posterior distribution over latent variables.

Core idea

Ordinary PCA chooses one orthogonal subspace to reconstruct all centered variance without using labels. dPCA first decomposes the balanced condition tensor into ANOVA-style marginalizations associated with time, task-variable main effects and interactions. It then solves a separate regularized reduced-rank regression for each marginalization: the full data are projected through a decoder, but the reconstruction target is only the selected label-dependent part of the data (Kobak et al. 2016).

This separates two roles that coincide in ordinary PCA. Decoder rows define low-dimensional readouts \(D_\phi X\); encoder columns map those readouts back toward \(X_\phi\). Allowing encoder and decoder axes to differ avoids forcing all task-associated subspaces to be mutually orthogonal.

Generative / modeling assumptions

The bounded 2016 dPCA formulation is a deterministic supervised approximation method, not a generative probability model. Its operative assumptions are:

  • declared task labels and their interactions provide scientifically relevant partitions of variation;
  • condition means can be compared in one common feature space;
  • linear readouts and linear reconstructions are adequate summaries;
  • squared Frobenius reconstruction loss and ridge regularization are appropriate criteria;
  • the factorial marginalization is meaningful under the declared balancing or weighting convention; and
  • enough independent trials exist to estimate condition means, regularization and held-out behavior.

Earlier dPCA work used an orthogonality-constrained probabilistic formulation, while the canonical reduced-rank formulation removed that constraint because task-related subspaces can be nonorthogonal (Brendel et al. 2011; Kobak et al. 2016). Neither formulation implies that the labeled effects are physical sources or that the data were generated by independent modules.

Mathematical formulation

Marginalizations

Let

\[ \Omega=\{t,a_1,\ldots,a_m\} \]

be the set of indexed variables. For each subset \(S\subseteq\Omega\), let \(\mathcal M_S X\) denote averaging over every variable in \(\Omega\setminus S\) and replicating the resulting values across the averaged indices. Define the full factorial decomposition recursively by

\[ X_S = \mathcal M_S X - \sum_{R\subsetneq S}X_R. \]

The empty-set term is the grand mean and is zero after global feature-wise centering. Under a complete balanced factorial design with uniform cell weighting,

\[ X=\sum_{S\subseteq\Omega}X_S, \]

and distinct terms are orthogonal in the Frobenius inner product, so their squared norms add (Brendel et al. 2011; Kobak et al. 2016).

Interactions are not residual nuisances to be discarded automatically. With stimulus \(s\), decision \(d\) and time \(t\), the complete decomposition contains \(t,s,d,ts,td,sd,tsd\). The canonical neural presentation groups terms such as

\[ X_{\text{stimulus}}=X_s+X_{ts}, \qquad X_{\text{decision}}=X_d+X_{td}, \]

and

\[ X_{\text{interaction}}=X_{sd}+X_{tsd}, \]

while \(X_t\) is condition-independent time variation. This grouping is an analyst-declared interpretive choice. Different groupings, label codings or averaging measures define different demixing targets.

Regularized condition-mean reduced-rank objective

For each grouped marginalization \(\phi\), choose rank \(q_\phi\) and solve

\[ \min_{F_\phi,D_\phi} \left\lVert X_\phi-F_\phi D_\phi X \right\rVert_F^2 + \lambda \left\lVert F_\phi D_\phi\right\rVert_F^2, \]

subject to

\[ \operatorname{rank}(F_\phi D_\phi)\le q_\phi. \]

Both the predictor \(X\) and target \(X_\phi\) in this equation are training condition means, with marginalized means replicated across the averaged indices. The published analysis selects regularization by holding out pseudo-trials and rebuilding training means; it also explicitly notes that the fitted PSTH formulation ignores trial-to-trial variability and unknown noise correlations (Kobak et al. 2016). A method that fits all individual trials or adds an explicit trial-noise covariance solves a different objective and is outside this bounded object.

A normalization convention such as

\[ F_\phi^\top F_\phi=I \]

removes arbitrary encoder–decoder scaling but not every degeneracy. The total loss is the sum of the separately minimized marginal losses.

Writing \(A_\phi=F_\phi D_\phi\), the unrestricted ridge-regression map is

\[ A_{\phi,\lambda} = X_\phi X^\top \left(XX^\top+\lambda I\right)^{-1}, \]

with a pseudoinverse or equivalent stable solver required at singular boundaries. Let \(U_{\phi,q}\) contain the leading left singular vectors of \(A_{\phi,\lambda}X\). One canonical factorization is

\[ F_\phi=U_{\phi,q}, \qquad D_\phi=U_{\phi,q}^\top A_{\phi,\lambda}. \]

Thus each marginal problem is a ridge-regularized reduced-rank regression followed by a spectral truncation (Kobak et al. 2016). Reduced-rank regression constrains the rank of a multivariate regression coefficient matrix and has a broader statistical theory independent of dPCA (Izenman 2008).

Variance accounting

For centered balanced data, define total and marginal variances using

\[ V_{\mathrm{tot}}=\lVert X\rVert_F^2, \qquad V_\phi=\lVert X_\phi\rVert_F^2. \]

The balanced orthogonal decomposition gives

\[ V_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_\phi V_\phi \]

only when all ungrouped terms and any declared residual/noise term are included consistently. For a decoder row \(d\), its projected variance attributed to marginalization \(\phi\) can be summarized by

\[ v_\phi(d)=\lVert dX_\phi\rVert_2^2, \]

and a descriptive demixing index is

\[ \operatorname{DI}(d) = \frac{\max_\phi v_\phi(d)}{\sum_\psi v_\psi(d)}. \]

This index is tied to the chosen labels, averaging and metric. Because decoder axes across marginalizations may be nonorthogonal, component-wise reconstructed or projected variances can overlap; they must not be summed as if dPCA were one orthogonal PCA basis. Reconstruction quality should instead be computed from the actual combined reconstruction or from explicitly defined marginal errors.

What is learned?

For each marginalization, dPCA learns a regularized low-rank linear map \(A_\phi=F_\phi D_\phi\), a representative encoder/decoder factorization, ordered component scores and marginal reconstructions. It also yields descriptive variance and demixing summaries under the fixed tensor decomposition.

It does not learn task labels, a causal graph, independent sources, neural modules, a transition law, a latent-state posterior or a unique biological coordinate system.

What is fixed?

The analyst fixes the population/features, trials and condition cells, alignment and preprocessing, centering and scaling, label set and coding, factorial averaging measure, interaction grouping, cell weighting or rebalancing, ranks, regularization-selection protocol, cross-validation unit, number of components displayed, and any classifier used for evaluation.

Trial averaging is also fixed. Fitting on condition means emphasizes repeatable label-associated structure but suppresses trial-to-trial variability. Applying decoders to single trials is a separate generalization question.

Temporal assumptions

Time is an indexed task variable in the bounded dPCA formulation. A time marginalization describes mean variation across aligned time bins, while terms such as stimulus–time describe label-dependent temporal profiles. The method does not estimate transitions, derivatives, process noise, a vector field or an evolution law.

Changing bin width, alignment event, smoothing kernel, time warping or analyzed interval changes the marginalizations. Smooth dPCA traces can arise from smoothing and trial averaging; they are not evidence for smooth latent dynamics. Treating adjacent bins from one trial as independent held-out samples causes leakage.

Geometry assumptions

dPCA uses linear projections and Euclidean/Frobenius squared error in the declared feature coordinates. Feature scaling therefore changes the objective and learned axes. Different marginalizations may occupy overlapping, oblique subspaces; the method does not impose or recover a manifold, nonlinear metric, topology or Riemannian geometry.

The encoder and decoder for one component need not point along the same feature-space axis, and decoders from different marginalizations need not be orthogonal. A two-dimensional plot combines selected linear readouts; its visual separation is not a coordinate-free geometric fact.

Supervision

dPCA is supervised because experimentally supplied labels determine \(X_\phi\) and therefore enter the training loss. Time bins also act as indexed design levels. The method may be unsupervised with respect to a later behavioral outcome only if that outcome was not used in labels, preprocessing, hyperparameter tuning or component selection.

Label correlation and deterministic relations among labels can make marginal effects inseparable or change their interpretation. For example, if a decision is nearly determined by a stimulus, assigning variance separately to stimulus and decision depends on the sampled error trials, coding, balancing and interaction convention. Supervision must not be hidden behind the term “dimensionality reduction.”

Optimization / inference

The canonical condition-mean objective decomposes into one regularized reduced-rank regression per marginalization. Each can be solved using a stable ridge-regression system and singular value decomposition. This is a deterministic fit rather than posterior inference and does not estimate trial-level noise covariance.

The ridge parameter \(\lambda\) and, when tuned, ranks \(q_\phi\) must be selected using training data only. Kobak et al. used held-out trials to form test pseudo-trials and selected regularization through repeated cross-validation; this supports the principle of trial-level separation, not one universally valid split or parameter grid (Kobak et al. 2016). Numerical choices include covariance regularization, pseudoinverse tolerance and handling of features with near-zero variance.

Strengths

  • It makes task labels and interactions explicit rather than asking unlabeled variance to align with experimental questions.
  • Separate encoder and decoder matrices permit nonorthogonal task-associated subspaces.
  • The reduced-rank objective balances marginal reconstruction with compactness.
  • Marginalizations provide an auditable accounting of which declared effects a component expresses.
  • Linear decoders can be applied to held-out trials when preprocessing and fitting are leakage-safe.
  • PCA, marginal averages and decoding provide clear adjacent baselines.

Failure modes

  • Unbalanced or missing cells: naive averaging can destroy orthogonality and confound main effects with interactions.
  • Correlated labels: stimulus, choice, reward and outcome may not be separately estimable under the experimental design.
  • Interaction reassignment: grouping time interactions with main task effects can produce a preferred narrative by construction.
  • Trial averaging: weak, heterogeneous or trial-specific structure may disappear, while smoothed means can look more reliable than single-trial readouts are.
  • Pseudo-populations: combining sequentially recorded units prevents estimation of simultaneous noise correlations and may overstate population-level availability.
  • Overlapping marginal subspaces: no linear axes may cleanly separate the selected effects; forced labeling of components does not repair overlap.
  • Overfitting: high feature count, few trials, many labels and flexible component selection can fit sampling noise.
  • Leakage: computing condition means, smoothing parameters, scaling, ranks, regularization or preferred components with held-out trials produces optimistic results.
  • Scale sensitivity: high-variance neurons or features dominate Frobenius loss unless scaling is scientifically justified.
  • Nonlinear structure: label dependence may not be representable through low-rank linear readouts.
  • Outliers and artifacts: variance-based fitting can prioritize abnormal trials or units.
  • Instability: close singular values, weak marginal variance or small perturbations can rotate the learned subspace.
  • Interpretive reification: high demixing index or decoding accuracy can be mistaken for independent neural channels, anatomical modules or causes.

Identifiability issues

Only the low-rank product \(A_\phi=F_\phi D_\phi\) enters the reconstruction. Without normalization, for any invertible \(R\),

\[ F_\phi D_\phi = (F_\phi R)(R^{-1}D_\phi), \]

so encoder and decoder factors are not separately identified. Choosing orthonormal encoder columns and ordering components selects a convention. Individual component signs remain arbitrary, and repeated singular values permit rotations within the corresponding fitted subspace.

Across marginalizations, axes are fitted separately and can be nonorthogonal. Correlated scores do not contradict the algorithm, and apparent visual separation does not establish statistical independence. Changing task coding, interaction grouping, cell weights, rank or regularization changes the estimand rather than revealing another view of one uniquely identified decomposition.

“Demixed” therefore means that a component concentrates variance from one declared marginalization under the fitted loss. It does not mean independent components, causal separation, unique neural modules, anatomical segregation, mechanistic variables or uniquely identified latent causes.

Comparison with simpler methods

  • PCA: fit a rank-matched PCA using the same centered and scaled training matrix. PCA provides the strongest ordinary linear reconstruction baseline but ignores labels.
  • Marginal averages and contrasts: plot or quantify \(X_\phi\) directly without learning a projection. This tests whether the substantive conclusion was already visible in condition means.
  • Separate PCA per marginalization: fit PCA to each \(X_\phi\). This compresses marginal means but does not by itself provide decoder axes that isolate that marginalization when applied to full or single-trial data.
  • Simple label decoding: fit a prespecified linear classifier or regressor to raw features or PCA scores. Decoding tests predictability of labels; it does not preserve the geometry or reconstruct the population activity and is not equivalent to demixing.
  • Mean-only reconstruction: reconstruct each feature by its training mean as a basic error reference.

A dPCA analysis should report whether it improves held-out marginal reconstruction or a separately declared scientific criterion, not merely whether its components look cleaner than PCA.

Evaluation criteria

A leakage-safe evaluation should include:

  1. Trial-level splitting: hold out complete trials, sessions, animals or other independent units as appropriate. Do not split adjacent time bins from the same trial across folds.
  2. Training-only preprocessing: compute centering, scaling, smoothing choices, condition means, marginalizations, interaction groupings, regularization, ranks and component ordering without test observations.
  3. Held-out marginal reconstruction: rebuild held-out pseudo-trials or held-out condition means from independent test trials, then evaluate \[ \left\lVert X^{\mathrm{test}}_\phi- F^{\mathrm{train}}_\phi D^{\mathrm{train}}_\phi X^{\mathrm{test}}\right\rVert_F^2 \] with the training definitions applied to those held-out condition estimates. This tests transfer of the mean-based map; it is not a trial-noise likelihood.
  4. Variance accounting: report total and marginal variance, combined reconstruction error, and the contribution of discarded or residual terms. Do not add overlapping nonorthogonal component contributions as though they were independent.
  5. Demixing diagnostics: report the full vector \(v_\phi(d)\) or a confusion matrix, not only the largest marginal label.
  6. Stability: compare subspaces and held-out conclusions across trial resamples, sessions, plausible ranks, regularization values and interaction groupings, using sign/subspace alignment fitted only where appropriate.
  7. Baselines: compare rank-matched PCA, raw marginal averages, separate marginal PCA and simple leakage-safe label decoding.
  8. Nulls: shuffle labels at the independent-trial or experimental-unit level while preserving the declared balance structure.

If decoding is reported, the decoder/component selection and classifier must be refitted inside every training fold. Successful decoding establishes label information under that protocol, not independent representation, causal role or mechanism.

Minimal toy model

A future non-executable toy model should generate balanced trials for time \(t\), binary stimulus \(s\in\{-1,+1\}\) and binary decision \(d\in\{-1,+1\}\):

\[ x(t,s,d,k) = a f_0(t) +b\,s f_s(t) +c\,d f_d(t) +e\,sd f_{sd}(t) +\varepsilon_k(t), \]

where \(a,b,c,e\in\mathbb R^N\) are known mixing vectors and \(\varepsilon\) is declared trial noise. The vectors \(b\) and \(c\) should be oblique rather than artificially orthogonal. Generation should retain the true time, stimulus, decision and interaction terms.

The future comparison should fit PCA, direct marginal averages, separate marginal PCA, a simple linear label decoder and dPCA using training trials only. Evaluation should measure held-out marginal reconstruction, component marginal-variance profiles, subspace recovery up to sign/rotation, and label decoding as a separate criterion.

Two negative controls are required: make \(b\) and \(c\) nearly collinear to demonstrate nonseparable marginal subspaces, and remove or severely unbalance one \((s,d)\) cell to demonstrate confounding and marginalization failure. A label-shuffle null should remove systematic task dependence. This contract creates no code, data or result in this Method Object.

Relevance to LDG

dPCA is LDG’s canonical supervised linear decomposition for separating declared task-variable contributions from ordinary variance maximization. It provides a controlled example of how labels, averaging, loss and experimental design define a representation—and why a useful representation must not be promoted into an independent latent generator or mechanism.

WarningWhat this does NOT imply

A component labeled “stimulus,” “decision,” “reward,” or “time” is label- and loss-dependent. dPCA does not establish statistical independence, causal separation, anatomical segregation, unique neural modules, a latent state, physical dynamics or mechanism. High explained variance, demixing index or decoding accuracy does not strengthen those claims automatically.

Mouse trajectory

No modality-specific empirical claim is retained. Behavioral factors, trial alignment and repeated measurements would have to define a balanced supervised tensor; components would remain label-dependent linear summaries rather than behavioral causes or dynamics.

Eye movement

No modality-specific empirical claim is retained. Event labels, gaze targets and time alignment can supervise a decomposition, but a target-associated component would not identify an oculomotor module or transition law.

EEG

No modality-specific empirical claim is retained. Rereferencing, channel scaling, subject/session structure and trial balance would determine the fitted marginalizations; sensor components would not automatically be neural sources.

MEG / neural population data

Kobak et al. introduced the canonical reduced-rank dPCA formulation and reanalyzed four neural population datasets spanning different species, recorded regions and tasks (Kobak et al. 2016). This is primary evidence that the method can summarize those specific trial-structured datasets. It does not establish universal demixed organization, anatomical modules or causal mechanisms in neural populations, and it does not directly validate MEG applications.

Primary sources

  • Machens develops marginalized covariance constructions for separating declared task-parameter variance and explicitly discusses overlap, nonseparability and the distinction from independent-component assumptions (Machens 2010).
  • Brendel, Romo and Machens introduce the earlier probabilistic dPCA formulation, including factorial marginalizations and interaction terms (Brendel et al. 2011).
  • Kobak et al. introduce the canonical encoder/decoder reduced-rank reconstruction objective, regularization, balancing treatment, cross-validation and neural applications used in this object (Kobak et al. 2016).

Reviews / secondary sources

  • Izenman provides an inspected authoritative treatment of multivariate reduced-rank regression, rank-constrained coefficient matrices and weighted least-squares formulations (Izenman 2008, chap. 6).

Open methodological questions

  • How should marginalizations be defined for continuous, hierarchical or strongly correlated task variables?
  • Which weighting estimand is appropriate when condition frequencies are scientifically meaningful but highly unequal?
  • How should uncertainty in condition means and marginal subspaces be propagated to component plots?
  • When should interactions remain separate instead of being grouped with time-dependent main effects?
  • How can simultaneous noise covariance be incorporated without treating pseudo-populations as simultaneous recordings?
  • Which conclusions remain stable under alternative label codings, ranks and regularization?

Both the predictor $X$ and target $X_\phi$ in this equation are training
condition means, with marginalized means replicated across the averaged
indices. The published analysis selects regularization by holding out
pseudo-trials and rebuilding training means; it also explicitly notes that the
fitted PSTH formulation ignores trial-to-trial variability and unknown noise
correlations [@KobakEtAl2016]. A method that fits all individual trials or adds
an explicit trial-noise covariance solves a different objective and is outside
this bounded object.

A normalization convention such as

$$
F_\phi^\top F_\phi=I
$$

removes arbitrary encoder--decoder scaling but not every degeneracy. The total loss is the sum of the separately minimized marginal losses.

Writing $A_\phi=F_\phi D_\phi$, the unrestricted ridge-regression map is

$$
A_{\phi,\lambda}
=
X_\phi X^\top
\left(XX^\top+\lambda I\right)^{-1},
$$

with a pseudoinverse or equivalent stable solver required at singular boundaries. Let $U_{\phi,q}$ contain the leading left singular vectors of $A_{\phi,\lambda}X$. One canonical factorization is

$$
F_\phi=U_{\phi,q},
\qquad
D_\phi=U_{\phi,q}^\top A_{\phi,\lambda}.
$$

Thus each marginal problem is a ridge-regularized reduced-rank regression followed by a spectral truncation [@KobakEtAl2016]. Reduced-rank regression constrains the rank of a multivariate regression coefficient matrix and has a broader statistical theory independent of dPCA [@Izenman2008].

### Variance accounting

For centered balanced data, define total and marginal variances using

$$
V_{\mathrm{tot}}=\lVert X\rVert_F^2,
\qquad
V_\phi=\lVert X_\phi\rVert_F^2.
$$

The balanced orthogonal decomposition gives

$$
V_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_\phi V_\phi
$$

only when all ungrouped terms and any declared residual/noise term are included consistently. For a decoder row $d$, its projected variance attributed to marginalization $\phi$ can be summarized by

$$
v_\phi(d)=\lVert dX_\phi\rVert_2^2,
$$

and a descriptive demixing index is

$$
\operatorname{DI}(d)
=
\frac{\max_\phi v_\phi(d)}{\sum_\psi v_\psi(d)}.
$$

This index is tied to the chosen labels, averaging and metric. Because decoder axes across marginalizations may be nonorthogonal, component-wise reconstructed or projected variances can overlap; they must not be summed as if dPCA were one orthogonal PCA basis. Reconstruction quality should instead be computed from the actual combined reconstruction or from explicitly defined marginal errors.

## What is learned?

For each marginalization, dPCA learns a regularized low-rank linear map $A_\phi=F_\phi D_\phi$, a representative encoder/decoder factorization, ordered component scores and marginal reconstructions. It also yields descriptive variance and demixing summaries under the fixed tensor decomposition.

It does not learn task labels, a causal graph, independent sources, neural modules, a transition law, a latent-state posterior or a unique biological coordinate system.

## What is fixed?

The analyst fixes the population/features, trials and condition cells, alignment and preprocessing, centering and scaling, label set and coding, factorial averaging measure, interaction grouping, cell weighting or rebalancing, ranks, regularization-selection protocol, cross-validation unit, number of components displayed, and any classifier used for evaluation.

Trial averaging is also fixed. Fitting on condition means emphasizes repeatable label-associated structure but suppresses trial-to-trial variability. Applying decoders to single trials is a separate generalization question.

## Temporal assumptions

Time is an indexed task variable in the bounded dPCA formulation. A time marginalization describes mean variation across aligned time bins, while terms such as stimulus--time describe label-dependent temporal profiles. The method does not estimate transitions, derivatives, process noise, a vector field or an evolution law.

Changing bin width, alignment event, smoothing kernel, time warping or analyzed interval changes the marginalizations. Smooth dPCA traces can arise from smoothing and trial averaging; they are not evidence for smooth latent dynamics. Treating adjacent bins from one trial as independent held-out samples causes leakage.

## Geometry assumptions

dPCA uses linear projections and Euclidean/Frobenius squared error in the declared feature coordinates. Feature scaling therefore changes the objective and learned axes. Different marginalizations may occupy overlapping, oblique subspaces; the method does not impose or recover a manifold, nonlinear metric, topology or Riemannian geometry.

The encoder and decoder for one component need not point along the same feature-space axis, and decoders from different marginalizations need not be orthogonal. A two-dimensional plot combines selected linear readouts; its visual separation is not a coordinate-free geometric fact.

## Supervision

dPCA is supervised because experimentally supplied labels determine $X_\phi$ and therefore enter the training loss. Time bins also act as indexed design levels. The method may be unsupervised with respect to a later behavioral outcome only if that outcome was not used in labels, preprocessing, hyperparameter tuning or component selection.

Label correlation and deterministic relations among labels can make marginal effects inseparable or change their interpretation. For example, if a decision is nearly determined by a stimulus, assigning variance separately to stimulus and decision depends on the sampled error trials, coding, balancing and interaction convention. Supervision must not be hidden behind the term “dimensionality reduction.”

## Optimization / inference

The canonical condition-mean objective decomposes into one regularized reduced-rank regression per marginalization. Each can be solved using a stable ridge-regression system and singular value decomposition. This is a deterministic fit rather than posterior inference and does not estimate trial-level noise covariance.

The ridge parameter $\lambda$ and, when tuned, ranks $q_\phi$ must be selected using training data only. Kobak et al. used held-out trials to form test pseudo-trials and selected regularization through repeated cross-validation; this supports the principle of trial-level separation, not one universally valid split or parameter grid [@KobakEtAl2016]. Numerical choices include covariance regularization, pseudoinverse tolerance and handling of features with near-zero variance.

## Strengths

- It makes task labels and interactions explicit rather than asking unlabeled variance to align with experimental questions.
- Separate encoder and decoder matrices permit nonorthogonal task-associated subspaces.
- The reduced-rank objective balances marginal reconstruction with compactness.
- Marginalizations provide an auditable accounting of which declared effects a component expresses.
- Linear decoders can be applied to held-out trials when preprocessing and fitting are leakage-safe.
- PCA, marginal averages and decoding provide clear adjacent baselines.

## Failure modes

- **Unbalanced or missing cells:** naive averaging can destroy orthogonality and confound main effects with interactions.
- **Correlated labels:** stimulus, choice, reward and outcome may not be separately estimable under the experimental design.
- **Interaction reassignment:** grouping time interactions with main task effects can produce a preferred narrative by construction.
- **Trial averaging:** weak, heterogeneous or trial-specific structure may disappear, while smoothed means can look more reliable than single-trial readouts are.
- **Pseudo-populations:** combining sequentially recorded units prevents estimation of simultaneous noise correlations and may overstate population-level availability.
- **Overlapping marginal subspaces:** no linear axes may cleanly separate the selected effects; forced labeling of components does not repair overlap.
- **Overfitting:** high feature count, few trials, many labels and flexible component selection can fit sampling noise.
- **Leakage:** computing condition means, smoothing parameters, scaling, ranks, regularization or preferred components with held-out trials produces optimistic results.
- **Scale sensitivity:** high-variance neurons or features dominate Frobenius loss unless scaling is scientifically justified.
- **Nonlinear structure:** label dependence may not be representable through low-rank linear readouts.
- **Outliers and artifacts:** variance-based fitting can prioritize abnormal trials or units.
- **Instability:** close singular values, weak marginal variance or small perturbations can rotate the learned subspace.
- **Interpretive reification:** high demixing index or decoding accuracy can be mistaken for independent neural channels, anatomical modules or causes.

## Identifiability issues

Only the low-rank product $A_\phi=F_\phi D_\phi$ enters the reconstruction. Without normalization, for any invertible $R$,

$$
F_\phi D_\phi
=
(F_\phi R)(R^{-1}D_\phi),
$$

so encoder and decoder factors are not separately identified. Choosing orthonormal encoder columns and ordering components selects a convention. Individual component signs remain arbitrary, and repeated singular values permit rotations within the corresponding fitted subspace.

Across marginalizations, axes are fitted separately and can be nonorthogonal. Correlated scores do not contradict the algorithm, and apparent visual separation does not establish statistical independence. Changing task coding, interaction grouping, cell weights, rank or regularization changes the estimand rather than revealing another view of one uniquely identified decomposition.

“Demixed” therefore means that a component concentrates variance from one declared marginalization under the fitted loss. It does **not** mean independent components, causal separation, unique neural modules, anatomical segregation, mechanistic variables or uniquely identified latent causes.

## Comparison with simpler methods

- **PCA:** fit a rank-matched PCA using the same centered and scaled training matrix. PCA provides the strongest ordinary linear reconstruction baseline but ignores labels.
- **Marginal averages and contrasts:** plot or quantify $X_\phi$ directly without learning a projection. This tests whether the substantive conclusion was already visible in condition means.
- **Separate PCA per marginalization:** fit PCA to each $X_\phi$. This compresses marginal means but does not by itself provide decoder axes that isolate that marginalization when applied to full or single-trial data.
- **Simple label decoding:** fit a prespecified linear classifier or regressor to raw features or PCA scores. Decoding tests predictability of labels; it does not preserve the geometry or reconstruct the population activity and is not equivalent to demixing.
- **Mean-only reconstruction:** reconstruct each feature by its training mean as a basic error reference.

A dPCA analysis should report whether it improves held-out marginal reconstruction or a separately declared scientific criterion, not merely whether its components look cleaner than PCA.

## Evaluation criteria

A leakage-safe evaluation should include:

1. **Trial-level splitting:** hold out complete trials, sessions, animals or other independent units as appropriate. Do not split adjacent time bins from the same trial across folds.
2. **Training-only preprocessing:** compute centering, scaling, smoothing choices, condition means, marginalizations, interaction groupings, regularization, ranks and component ordering without test observations.
3. **Held-out marginal reconstruction:** rebuild held-out pseudo-trials or
   held-out condition means from independent test trials, then evaluate
   $$
   \left\lVert X^{\mathrm{test}}_\phi-
   F^{\mathrm{train}}_\phi D^{\mathrm{train}}_\phi
   X^{\mathrm{test}}\right\rVert_F^2
   $$
   with the training definitions applied to those held-out condition estimates.
   This tests transfer of the mean-based map; it is not a trial-noise likelihood.
4. **Variance accounting:** report total and marginal variance, combined reconstruction error, and the contribution of discarded or residual terms. Do not add overlapping nonorthogonal component contributions as though they were independent.
5. **Demixing diagnostics:** report the full vector $v_\phi(d)$ or a confusion matrix, not only the largest marginal label.
6. **Stability:** compare subspaces and held-out conclusions across trial resamples, sessions, plausible ranks, regularization values and interaction groupings, using sign/subspace alignment fitted only where appropriate.
7. **Baselines:** compare rank-matched PCA, raw marginal averages, separate marginal PCA and simple leakage-safe label decoding.
8. **Nulls:** shuffle labels at the independent-trial or experimental-unit level while preserving the declared balance structure.

If decoding is reported, the decoder/component selection and classifier must be refitted inside every training fold. Successful decoding establishes label information under that protocol, not independent representation, causal role or mechanism.

## Minimal toy model

A future non-executable toy model should generate balanced trials for time $t$, binary stimulus $s\in\{-1,+1\}$ and binary decision $d\in\{-1,+1\}$:

$$
x(t,s,d,k)
=
a f_0(t)
+b\,s f_s(t)
+c\,d f_d(t)
+e\,sd f_{sd}(t)
+\varepsilon_k(t),
$$

where $a,b,c,e\in\mathbb R^N$ are known mixing vectors and $\varepsilon$ is declared trial noise. The vectors $b$ and $c$ should be oblique rather than artificially orthogonal. Generation should retain the true time, stimulus, decision and interaction terms.

The future comparison should fit PCA, direct marginal averages, separate marginal PCA, a simple linear label decoder and dPCA using training trials only. Evaluation should measure held-out marginal reconstruction, component marginal-variance profiles, subspace recovery up to sign/rotation, and label decoding as a separate criterion.

Two negative controls are required: make $b$ and $c$ nearly collinear to demonstrate nonseparable marginal subspaces, and remove or severely unbalance one $(s,d)$ cell to demonstrate confounding and marginalization failure. A label-shuffle null should remove systematic task dependence. This contract creates no code, data or result in this Method Object.

## Relevance to LDG

dPCA is LDG's canonical supervised linear decomposition for separating declared task-variable contributions from ordinary variance maximization. It provides a controlled example of how labels, averaging, loss and experimental design define a representation—and why a useful representation must not be promoted into an independent latent generator or mechanism.

::: {.callout-warning title="What this does NOT imply"}
A component labeled “stimulus,” “decision,” “reward,” or “time” is label- and loss-dependent. dPCA does not establish statistical independence, causal separation, anatomical segregation, unique neural modules, a latent state, physical dynamics or mechanism. High explained variance, demixing index or decoding accuracy does not strengthen those claims automatically.
:::

### Mouse trajectory

No modality-specific empirical claim is retained. Behavioral factors, trial alignment and repeated measurements would have to define a balanced supervised tensor; components would remain label-dependent linear summaries rather than behavioral causes or dynamics.

### Eye movement

No modality-specific empirical claim is retained. Event labels, gaze targets and time alignment can supervise a decomposition, but a target-associated component would not identify an oculomotor module or transition law.

### EEG

No modality-specific empirical claim is retained. Rereferencing, channel scaling, subject/session structure and trial balance would determine the fitted marginalizations; sensor components would not automatically be neural sources.

### MEG / neural population data

Kobak et al. introduced the canonical reduced-rank dPCA formulation and reanalyzed four neural population datasets spanning different species, recorded regions and tasks [@KobakEtAl2016]. This is primary evidence that the method can summarize those specific trial-structured datasets. It does not establish universal demixed organization, anatomical modules or causal mechanisms in neural populations, and it does not directly validate MEG applications.

## Primary sources

- Machens develops marginalized covariance constructions for separating declared task-parameter variance and explicitly discusses overlap, nonseparability and the distinction from independent-component assumptions [@Machens2010].
- Brendel, Romo and Machens introduce the earlier probabilistic dPCA formulation, including factorial marginalizations and interaction terms [@BrendelRomoMachens2011].
- Kobak et al. introduce the canonical encoder/decoder reduced-rank reconstruction objective, regularization, balancing treatment, cross-validation and neural applications used in this object [@KobakEtAl2016].

## Reviews / secondary sources

- Izenman provides an inspected authoritative treatment of multivariate reduced-rank regression, rank-constrained coefficient matrices and weighted least-squares formulations [@Izenman2008, chap. 6].

## Open methodological questions

- How should marginalizations be defined for continuous, hierarchical or strongly correlated task variables?
- Which weighting estimand is appropriate when condition frequencies are scientifically meaningful but highly unequal?
- How should uncertainty in condition means and marginal subspaces be propagated to component plots?
- When should interactions remain separate instead of being grouped with time-dependent main effects?
- How can simultaneous noise covariance be incorporated without treating pseudo-populations as simultaneous recordings?
- Which conclusions remain stable under alternative label codings, ranks and regularization?
