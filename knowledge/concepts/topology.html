Topology

Topology

One-sentence definition

A topology specifies which subsets of a set are open, thereby defining neighborhoods, continuity, convergence, and connectedness without first assigning numerical distances.

Why this concept matters

Topology describes qualitative structure preserved under continuous deformation. It is weaker than metric or Riemannian geometry: spaces can share a topology while having different distances, angles, and curvature.

Intuition

Topology records which points can be approached continuously and which regions are connected. It does not say how far apart points are.

Formal definition

A topology \(\mathcal T\) on a set \(X\) is a family of subsets such that:

  1. \(\varnothing,X\in\mathcal T\);
  2. arbitrary unions of members of \(\mathcal T\) belong to \(\mathcal T\);
  3. finite intersections of members of \(\mathcal T\) belong to \(\mathcal T\).

The pair \((X,\mathcal T)\) is a topological space. A map is continuous when preimages of open sets are open. Sutherland gives these definitions and distinguishes topology induced by a metric (Sutherland 2024).

Core notation

  • \(X\): underlying set.
  • \(\mathcal T\): topology.
  • \(U\in\mathcal T\): open set.
  • \(f:X\to Y\): continuous map when open-set preimages are open.

Prerequisites

Relationship to adjacent concepts

Concept Distinction
Metric Assigns numerical distances and induces a topology; different metrics can induce the same topology.
Geometry Adds quantitative length, angle, or curvature beyond topology.
Manifold A topological space with local Euclidean structure; a smooth manifold adds compatible smooth charts.
Graph connectivity A discrete combinatorial relation, not automatically the topology of an underlying state space.

What this does NOT imply

A topology does not imply a metric, smoothness, manifold structure, a unique embedding, low dimension, curvature, or dynamical law.

Minimal example

The usual open intervals generate the standard topology on \(\mathbb R\). The discrete topology instead declares every subset open. The same underlying set can therefore support different topologies.

Neuroscience / behavioral relevance

Claims about loops, connected components, or holes in latent data are topological claims. They require sampling and noise analysis and are not established by one visualization or clustering result.

Relevance to LDG

Topology supplies the precise layer beneath manifolds, circles, and tori while keeping topology distinct from metric geometry.

Open questions

  • Which topological features are stable across scales and noise?
  • How should sampling uncertainty affect claims about connectedness or holes?
  • Which null models are appropriate for topological summaries?
