Observation Noise

Observation Noise

One-sentence definition

Observation noise is stochastic variability in measurements conditional on the modeled latent state and observation mapping.

Why this concept matters

Observation noise can make identical latent states produce different data without changing the underlying trajectory. Confusing it with process noise changes inference, prediction, and scientific interpretation.

Intuition

The system may be in the same state, but the sensor reports slightly different values each time.

Formal definition

In a probabilistic observation model, observation noise or measurement variability is encoded in the conditional observation kernel

\[ K_{k,\theta_y}(x,u,B) = \Pr(Y_k\in B\mid X_k=x,u_k=u,\theta_y), \qquad B\in\Sigma_{\mathcal Y}. \]

It changes the distribution of measurements while conditioning on the same modeled state and declared input. A deterministic observation map is the special case in which \(K_k\) is a Dirac measure.

Conditioned additive special case

A common representation is

\[ Y_k=G_k(X_k,u_k;\theta_y)+v_k, \]

with

\[ v_k\mid X_k=x,u_k=u \sim p_v(\,\cdot\mid x,u,k). \]

When the required moments exist, define

\[ m^v_k(x,u)=\mathbb E[v_k\mid x,u], \]

\[ \Sigma^v_k(x,u) = \operatorname{Cov}(v_k\mid x,u). \]

The familiar fixed-law model

\[ v_k\mid X_k,u_k\sim\mathcal N(0,R) \]

adds zero mean, Gaussianity, constant covariance, and conditional independence from state and input. These are optional assumptions, not the definition of observation noise. Tipping and Bishop’s PPCA model uses an isotropic Gaussian observation error as one special case (Tipping and Bishop 1999, secs. 2.1, 3.1).

Dependence and covariance structure

The following properties are separate:

  • Unbiased/zero conditional mean: \(m^v_k(x,u)=0\).
  • Homoscedastic: the conditional covariance does not vary with state, input, or time.
  • Heteroscedastic: \(\Sigma^v_k(x,u)\) varies with state, input, or time.
  • Uncorrelated channels: off-diagonal conditional covariance entries vanish.
  • Independent channels: the conditional joint law factorizes across channels; this is stronger than zero covariance outside a suitable Gaussian assumption.
  • White/temporally uncorrelated: under a declared conditioning path, \(\operatorname{Cov}(v_k,v_\ell\mid X_{0:T},u_{0:T},\theta_y)=0\) for \(k\ne\ell\).
  • Independent across time: under the same conditioning convention, the conditional joint law factorizes across times; this is stronger than uncorrelatedness outside the Gaussian case.
  • iid: under the same conditioning convention, combines independence across time with an identical conditional distribution.

A diagonal covariance supports conditional channel independence only under additional distributional assumptions such as joint Gaussianity. Tipping and Bishop make channel independence a consequence of their diagonal Gaussian error model, not covariance diagonality alone (Tipping and Bishop 1999, sec. 2.1).

Observation memory and temporal filtering

If a sensor has temporal memory, the observation may depend on latent history,

\[ p(y_k\mid x_{0:k},y_{0:k-1},\theta_y), \]

rather than only on \(x_k\). Separately, a colored stochastic error sequence can correlate measurements across time even without deterministic sensor filtering. These are distinct mechanisms, but either can invalidate the usual product \(\prod_k p(y_k\mid x_k)\) unless sensor memory or correlated noise is explicitly represented in the state or observation model.

Non-additive and non-Gaussian observation laws

NoteEstablished Statistical Background

Many observations do not have a natural additive-error representation. Examples include

\[ Y_k\mid X_k=x \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda(x)) \]

for counts and

\[ Y_k\mid X_k=x \sim \operatorname{Categorical}(\pi(x)) \]

for labels. Their variability is specified directly by the conditional observation law. Treating such data as Gaussian additive noise can change support, variance structure, and scientific interpretation.

Core notation

  • \(K_{k,\theta_y}(x,u,B)\): conditional observation probability kernel.
  • \(v_k\): additive observation-noise realization.
  • \(m^v_k(x,u)\): conditional observation-noise mean.
  • \(\Sigma^v_k(x,u)\): conditional observation-noise covariance.
  • \(R\): constant covariance in a common Gaussian special case.
  • \(G_k\): deterministic part of an additive observation mapping.

Prerequisites

What this does NOT imply

Observation noise does not imply zero mean, whiteness, independence, identical distribution, Gaussianity, additivity, homoscedasticity, unbiasedness, or state independence. Labeling residuals as noise does not prove they lack structure. Fitting or increasing a covariance \(R\) can absorb discrepancies and improve predictive calibration without identifying a physical sensor mechanism or separating noise from misspecification.

Minimal example

For

\[ X_{k+1}=X_k, \qquad Y_k=X_k+v_k, \qquad v_k\mid X_k\sim\mathcal N(0,R), \]

the latent state is constant while repeated measurements vary. Averaging reduces variance only under assumptions about independence or temporal correlation; it does not alter the state dynamics.

For a count observation,

\[ Y_k\mid X_k=x\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(e^x), \]

so \(\operatorname{Var}(Y_k\mid x)=\mathbb E[Y_k\mid x]=e^x\). The measurement variability is heteroscedastic and encoded by the count likelihood rather than an additive constant-variance Gaussian term.

Neuroscience / behavioral relevance

Sensor noise, spike-count variability, motion artifacts, and annotation errors may enter observation models differently. Treating all unexplained variance as independent observation noise can hide structured neural or behavioral signals.

Relevance to LDG

At L2, Observation Noise locates stochastic variability in the conditional measurement law and makes its moment, dependence, support, and memory assumptions explicit. It protects latent-dynamics claims from being driven by measurement variability and prepares deconvolution and state-space inference questions.

Open questions

  • Which noise correlations reflect sensors versus shared unmodeled state?
  • How should heteroscedastic or count-valued observations be modeled?
  • What calibration data constrain \(R\)?

Labeling residuals as noise does not prove they lack structure. Fitting or increasing a covariance $R$ can absorb discrepancies and improve predictive calibration without identifying a physical sensor mechanism or separating noise from misspecification.
