Linear Subspace

One-sentence definition

A linear subspace is a subset of a vector space that contains zero and is closed under vector addition and scalar multiplication.

Why this concept matters

Linear subspaces formalize directions of variation that can be combined linearly. They underlie PCA-style representations, linear dynamical modes, tangent-space approximations, and simple baselines for nonlinear geometry.

Intuition

A plane through the origin in \(\mathbb R^3\) is a subspace: adding two vectors in the plane or scaling either one keeps the result in the plane.

Formal definition

For a vector space \(V\), a subset \(U\subseteq V\) is a subspace when

\[ 0\in U,\qquad u+v\in U,\qquad au\in U \]

for all \(u,v\in U\) and scalars \(a\). A basis of \(U\) is a linearly independent spanning set; when \(U\) is finite-dimensional, the number of vectors in any basis is \(\dim U\). Axler gives these definitions and closure tests (Axler 2024, secs. 1C, 2C).

Core notation

  • \(V\): ambient vector space.
  • \(U\le V\): linear subspace.
  • \(\operatorname{span}\{v_1,\ldots,v_d\}\): subspace generated by vectors.
  • \(\dim U\): subspace dimension.

Relationship to adjacent concepts

Manifold Can be curved and need not be closed under linear combinations.
Tangent space A linear space attached locally to a smooth object, not necessarily the object itself.
Principal-component subspace A data-estimated subspace selected by a variance criterion.

What this does NOT imply

A fitted linear subspace does not imply a unique representation, mechanistic axes, globally linear dynamics, manifold structure, or that residual variation is noise.

Minimal example
