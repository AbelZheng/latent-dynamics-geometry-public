Latent Variable

Latent Variable

One-sentence definition

A latent variable is a variable included in a model but not directly observed in the available data and related to observations through the model; it may be inferred conditionally or integrated out.

Why this concept matters

Latent variables can provide compact explanations of dependence, hidden state, unmeasured causes, or structured variability. Their meaning comes from a specified model and evidence, not from being low-dimensional or visually interpretable.

Intuition

Suppose several measurements covary because the model posits a smaller set of unobserved quantities that influence them. Those unobserved quantities are latent variables. Different models can assign different latent variables to the same observations.

Formal definition

Let \(y\) denote observed data and \(z\) a latent variable. A latent-variable model specifies a joint or conditional structure such as

\[ p(y,z)=p(y\mid z)\,p(z), \]

while only \(y\) is directly available. Depending on the task, the model may integrate over \(z\) to obtain a marginal distribution \(p(y)\), infer \(p(z\mid y)\), or report a point summary of that conditional distribution.

In the inspected probabilistic PCA model,

\[ y=Wz+\mu+\varepsilon, \]

where \(z\) is latent, \(W\) links latent and observed variables, and \(\varepsilon\) is an observation-error term (Tipping and Bishop 1999). This is one example, not the definition of every latent variable.

Core notation

  • \(y\): observed variable or data vector.
  • \(z\): latent or unobserved variable.
  • \(p(z)\): prior or population model for the latent variable.
  • \(p(y\mid z)\): observation model.
  • \(p(z\mid y)\): inferred posterior distribution.
  • \(W\): one possible mapping from latent to observed coordinates.

Prerequisites

Assumed background: elementary probability notation. No existing LDG Knowledge Object is required.

Relationship to adjacent concepts

Concept Distinction
State A state is defined by its role in continuing a system description. A latent variable is defined by being unobserved in a model. A latent variable may represent state, a parameter, a cause, or another hidden quantity.
Observation Observations are available measurements; latent variables are inferred through a model relating them to observations.
Feature or representation A computed feature can be directly observed from data after transformation. Calling it latent requires a generative or inferential role, not merely compression.
Low-dimensional coordinate Latent variables can be high-dimensional, and a low-dimensional embedding can be deterministic rather than latent-variable inference.
Ground-truth cause A fitted latent variable need not correspond uniquely to a physical or biological cause.

What this does NOT imply

A latent variable does not imply:

  • that it is a true hidden mechanism or uniquely identifiable quantity;
  • low dimensionality, smooth geometry, or dynamical state;
  • that decoding or reconstruction validates its interpretation;
  • that different fitted coordinate systems represent different mechanisms;
  • causal influence on observations; or
  • that residual variation is meaningless noise.

Minimal example

Let \(z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\) and

\[ y_1=z+\varepsilon_1,\qquad y_2=2z+\varepsilon_2. \]

The observed variables \(y_1,y_2\) are measured; \(z\) is latent. Their correlation can support the model, but finite observations generally do not reveal a unique realized \(z\) or prove that this particular hidden variable generated the data.

Neuroscience / behavioral relevance

Neural population analyses often use latent variables to summarize shared structure across many recorded units (Cunningham and Yu 2014). Such variables can support prediction and hypothesis formation, but a low-dimensional representation is not by itself evidence of a neural mechanism.

Relevance to LDG

Latent Variable begins the bridge from dynamical state concepts to observation and inference. It gives LDG a place to separate model-hidden quantities from measured variables, geometric coordinates, and mechanistic claims.

Open questions

  • Which transformations of a latent variable preserve the same scientific interpretation?
  • When should a latent variable be interpreted as dynamical state rather than a static factor?
  • What interventions or cross-condition tests can constrain its meaning?

References

  • Tipping and Bishop give an inspected primary example of a probabilistic latent-variable model relating unobserved variables to observations (Tipping and Bishop 1999).
  • Cunningham and Yu review latent variables in neural population dimensionality reduction and caution that population representations must be connected to testable mechanisms (Cunningham and Yu 2014).
