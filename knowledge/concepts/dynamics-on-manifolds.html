Dynamics on Manifolds

Dynamics on Manifolds

One-sentence definition

Dynamics on a manifold evolve states within a manifold using a rule whose instantaneous vectors lie in the appropriate tangent spaces.

Why this concept matters

Many systems have constrained or non-Euclidean state spaces. Manifold dynamics make those constraints part of the state description rather than relying on ambient coordinates that may leave the admissible space.

Intuition

For motion constrained to a sphere, the instantaneous velocity must be tangent to the sphere. A vector pointing radially outward would leave the state space.

Formal definition

Let \(M\) be a smooth manifold. An autonomous vector field is a smooth section

\[ X:M\to TM, \qquad X(p)\in T_pM. \]

An integral curve is a smooth map \(\gamma:I\to M\) satisfying intrinsically

\[ \dot\gamma(t)=X(\gamma(t)). \]

In a chart \(x=(x^1,\ldots,x^n)\) with

\[ X=X^i(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}, \]

the integral-curve equation becomes

\[ \dot x^i(t)=X^i(x(t)). \]

A smooth vector field therefore supplies ordinary differential equations in each chart, with compatible expressions on overlaps.

Local flow and supported composition

Under the standard local existence and uniqueness assumptions, integral curves define a smooth local flow

\[ \Phi:\mathcal D\subseteq\mathbb R\times M\to M, \]

where \(\mathcal D\) is an open supported domain containing \(\{0\}\times M\). It satisfies

\[ \Phi(0,p)=p, \]

and

\[ \Phi(t+s,p)=\Phi(t,\Phi(s,p)) \]

whenever both sides are supported. Completeness is the additional condition \(\mathcal D=\mathbb R\times M\); tangency and smoothness alone do not imply it (Mrowka 2004, sec. 20.1; Teschl 2012, ch. 6).

Chart covariance

If \(y=h(x)\) is a smooth coordinate change, the vector-field components transform by

\[ Y^a(y) = \frac{\partial h^a}{\partial x^i}(x)X^i(x), \qquad x=h^{-1}(y). \]

Thus coordinate formulas can look different while representing the same intrinsic vector field and integral curves. A non-invertible projection or learned representation does not automatically preserve the vector field.

Embedded constraints and tangency

Suppose an embedded state manifold is a regular level set

\[ M=\{q\in\mathbb R^D:c(q)=0\}, \]

where \(c:\mathbb R^D\to\mathbb R^r\) is smooth and \(Dc_q\) has full rank on \(M\). Then

\[ T_qM=\ker Dc_q. \]

An ambient vector field \(F:\mathbb R^D\to\mathbb R^D\) is tangent along \(M\) when

\[ Dc_qF(q)=0, \qquad q\in M. \]

Under local uniqueness, restricting such a smooth tangent field to \(M\) produces manifold-valued integral curves. The algebraic constraint and tangency condition do not by themselves establish global existence, boundary viability, numerical preservation, or adequacy of \(M\) as the real state space.

Numerical projection and retraction

An ambient numerical update such as

\[ q_{n+1}=\Pi_M(q_n+hF(q_n)) \]

or a retraction update

\[ q_{n+1}=R_{q_n}(hX(q_n)) \]

can keep iterates on or near \(M\). These are discretization schemes, not the exact flow \(\Phi_h\). Projection/retraction choice can alter the dynamics; consistency, order, constraint error, invariant preservation, and convergence must be evaluated. A plotted constraint-preserving trajectory does not prove that the numerical map approximates the intended intrinsic vector field.

Additional geometric structures

A manifold and tangent vector field define dynamics without a metric. A Riemannian metric can define gradient dynamics \(X=-\operatorname{grad}_gV\) or other metric-dependent rules. Geodesic motion is a special second-order/connection-governed evolution, not a generic first-order vector field. Hamiltonian dynamics requires symplectic or Poisson structure, not merely a Riemannian metric. Each adjective adds assumptions and conserved/variational structure that must be declared.

Core notation

  • \(M\): state manifold.
  • \(T_pM\): tangent space.
  • \(X\in\Gamma(TM)\): smooth tangent vector field/section.
  • \(\gamma(t)\in M\): integral curve.
  • \(\mathcal D\subseteq\mathbb R\times M\): local flow domain.
  • \(\Phi_t:D_t\to M\): fixed-time local flow map; \(D_t=M\) only under the relevant global-existence condition.
  • \(c(q)=0\): embedded regular-level constraint.
  • \(Dc_qF(q)=0\): ambient tangency condition.
  • \(\Pi_M,R_q\): projection and retraction used by numerical updates.

Prerequisites

Flow supplies finite-time evolution. Manifold supplies the state space. Tangent Space supplies admissible instantaneous directions.

Relationship to adjacent concepts

Concept Distinction
Dynamics in ambient coordinates Represents manifold dynamics only when the field is tangent and the ambient coordinates/constraints correctly represent the state manifold.
Projected/retracted integrator A numerical map designed to respect constraints; it is not the exact local flow and requires consistency/error analysis.

What this does NOT imply

Dynamics on a manifold do not imply geodesic motion, known metric or curvature, global existence, Hamiltonian or gradient structure, low-dimensional truth, identifiable coordinates, numerical invariant preservation, or that a curved embedding reveals the generator. Tangency in an assumed embedding does not empirically prove the manifold or shared vector field.

Minimal example

On \(S^1\), the vector field \(\dot\theta=\omega\) generates constant angular motion. In the embedding \((x,y)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\), the velocity \((-\omega y,\omega x)\) is tangent to the circle.

Neuroscience / behavioral relevance

Circular head direction, orientation, or joint periodic latent states may motivate manifold-constrained dynamics. Evidence must separately support the state topology and the dynamical rule.

Relevance to LDG

At L2, Dynamics on Manifolds completes the spine by connecting intrinsic vector fields, chart equations, local flows, embedded tangency constraints, and numerical representations without collapsing manifold evidence, geometry, and dynamical recovery into one claim.

# Dynamics on Manifolds

## One-sentence definition

**Dynamics on a manifold** evolve states within a manifold using a rule whose instantaneous vectors lie in the appropriate tangent spaces.

## Why this concept matters

Many systems have constrained or non-Euclidean state spaces. Manifold dynamics make those constraints part of the state description rather than relying on ambient coordinates that may leave the admissible space.

## Intuition

For motion constrained to a sphere, the instantaneous velocity must be tangent to the sphere. A vector pointing radially outward would leave the state space.

## Formal definition

Let $M$ be a smooth manifold. An autonomous vector field is a smooth section

$$
X:M\to TM,
\qquad
X(p)\in T_pM.
$$

An integral curve is a smooth map $\gamma:I\to M$ satisfying intrinsically

$$
\dot\gamma(t)=X(\gamma(t)).
$$

In a chart $x=(x^1,\ldots,x^n)$ with

$$
X=X^i(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i},
$$

the integral-curve equation becomes

$$
\dot x^i(t)=X^i(x(t)).
$$

A smooth vector field therefore supplies ordinary differential equations in each chart, with compatible expressions on overlaps.

### Local flow and supported composition

Under the standard local existence and uniqueness assumptions, integral curves define a smooth local flow

$$
\Phi:\mathcal D\subseteq\mathbb R\times M\to M,
$$

where $\mathcal D$ is an open supported domain containing $\{0\}\times M$. It satisfies

$$
\Phi(0,p)=p,
$$

and

$$
\Phi(t+s,p)=\Phi(t,\Phi(s,p))
$$

whenever both sides are supported. Completeness is the additional condition $\mathcal D=\mathbb R\times M$; tangency and smoothness alone do not imply it [@Mrowka2004, sec. 20.1; @Teschl2012, ch. 6].

### Chart covariance

If $y=h(x)$ is a smooth coordinate change, the vector-field components transform by

$$
Y^a(y)
=
\frac{\partial h^a}{\partial x^i}(x)X^i(x),
\qquad x=h^{-1}(y).
$$

Thus coordinate formulas can look different while representing the same intrinsic vector field and integral curves. A non-invertible projection or learned representation does not automatically preserve the vector field.

### Embedded constraints and tangency

$$
M=\{q\in\mathbb R^D:c(q)=0\},
$$

where $c:\mathbb R^D\to\mathbb R^r$ is smooth and $Dc_q$ has full rank on $M$. Then

$$
T_qM=\ker Dc_q.
$$

An ambient vector field $F:\mathbb R^D\to\mathbb R^D$ is tangent along $M$ when

$$
Dc_qF(q)=0,
\qquad q\in M.
$$

### Numerical projection and retraction

$$

$$

$$
