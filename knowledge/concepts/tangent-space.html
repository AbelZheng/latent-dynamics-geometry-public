Tangent Space

Tangent Space

One-sentence definition

The tangent space \(T_pM\) is the vector space of first-order directions available on a smooth manifold \(M\) at a point \(p\).

Why this concept matters

The tangent space is the local linear arena for velocities, derivatives, vector fields, and Riemannian inner products. It approximates first-order behavior at one point; it is not the manifold itself.

Intuition

At a point on a sphere, the tangent plane contains the instantaneous directions in which a curve on the sphere can move. The sphere leaves that plane at second order.

Formal definition

Let \(M\) be a smooth \(n\)-manifold and \(p\in M\).

Curve-velocity definition

Consider smooth curves \(\gamma:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\to M\) with \(\gamma(0)=p\). Two curves are tangent-equivalent at \(p\) when, in one—and therefore every—chart \((U,x)\) containing \(p\),

\[ \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}x(\gamma_1(t)) = \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}x(\gamma_2(t)). \]

A tangent vector is an equivalence class \([\gamma]\), and the tangent space \(T_pM\) is the set of such classes with vector-space operations induced in coordinates. Mrowka gives the equivalent chart-representative construction (Mrowka 2004, sec. 7.2).

Derivation definition

Equivalently, a tangent vector at \(p\) is a linear map

\[ v:C^\infty_p(M)\to\mathbb R \]

on germs of smooth functions satisfying the Leibniz rule

\[ v(fg)=f(p)v(g)+g(p)v(f). \]

For a curve representative,

\[ v(f) = \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}f(\gamma(t)). \]

The curve-velocity and derivation descriptions define canonically isomorphic tangent spaces (Lee 1997, ch. 2). This Concept states the equivalence but defers its proof.

Coordinates and basis changes

A chart \(x=(x^1,\ldots,x^n)\) supplies the coordinate basis

\[ \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^1}\right|_p, \ldots, \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^n}\right|_p, \]

so every \(v\in T_pM\) has components

\[ v = v^i\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p. \]

Thus \(\dim T_pM=n\). Under another chart \(y=(y^1,\ldots,y^n)\),

\[ v'^a = \frac{\partial y^a}{\partial x^i}(p)v^i, \]

and

\[ \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p = \frac{\partial y^a}{\partial x^i}(p) \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial y^a}\right|_p. \]

The components change, while the intrinsic tangent vector does not.

Differential of a smooth map

For a smooth map \(f:M\to N\), the differential at \(p\) is the linear map

\[ df_p:T_pM\to T_{f(p)}N. \]

Using curve representatives,

\[ df_p([\gamma])=[f\circ\gamma]. \]

Using derivations,

\[ (df_pv)(g)=v(g\circ f). \]

In charts, the matrix of \(df_p\) is the Jacobian \([\partial f^a/\partial x^i]_p\) (Mrowka 2004, sec. 7.2).

Embedded tangent spaces

If \(i:M\hookrightarrow\widetilde M\) is a smooth embedding, \(di_p\) identifies \(T_pM\) with a linear subspace of \(T_{i(p)}\widetilde M\). For an embedded manifold in \(\mathbb R^D\), this gives the familiar ambient tangent plane. This represented subspace depends on the embedding, whereas the abstract \(T_pM\) is intrinsic. An arbitrary ambient subspace fitted to data is not automatically the image of an intrinsic tangent space.

Tangent bundle and vector fields

The tangent bundle is the disjoint union

\[ TM=\bigsqcup_{p\in M}T_pM \]

with its compatible smooth bundle structure. A smooth vector field is a smooth section \(X:M\to TM\) satisfying \(X(p)\in T_pM\). Bundle construction proofs and connection theory are deferred.

Core notation

  • \(T_pM\): tangent space at \(p\).
  • \(v\in T_pM\): tangent vector.
  • \([\gamma]\): equivalence class of curves through \(p\).
  • \(v(f)\): derivation of a smooth function germ.
  • \(\partial/\partial x^i|_p\): chart-induced tangent basis.
  • \(v^i\): tangent-vector components in a chart.
  • \(df_p:T_pM\to T_{f(p)}N\): differential/pushforward of a smooth map.
  • \(TM=\bigsqcup_{p\in M}T_pM\): tangent bundle.

Prerequisites

Manifold supplies charts and smooth curves. Linear Subspace supplies vector-space language.

Relationship to adjacent concepts

Concept Distinction
Manifold The nonlinear space of points; \(T_pM\) is one attached local vector space.
Ambient space An embedding differential represents \(T_pM\) as a subspace of the ambient tangent space; the intrinsic tangent space is not defined by ambient coordinates alone.
Differential / Jacobian \(df_p\) maps tangent vectors between manifolds. A coordinate Jacobian is its matrix representation, not the intrinsic map itself.
Tangent bundle The smoothly organized union of all tangent spaces; it is not one vector space because fibers at different points are distinct.
Vector field Assigns one tangent vector to each point, rather than describing one tangent space.
Local PCA plane A statistical estimate that may approximate a tangent space under additional sampling and noise assumptions.

What this does NOT imply

A tangent space does not imply global flatness, a canonical identification between different points, a unique ambient orientation, a metric or inner product, accurate estimation from sparse samples, validity far from \(p\), or that local linear dynamics extend globally.

Minimal example

For \(S^1\subset\mathbb R^2\) and \(p=(1,0)\),

\[ T_pS^1=\{(0,a):a\in\mathbb R\}. \]

This vertical line captures instantaneous motion at \(p\) but not the whole circle.

Neuroscience / behavioral relevance

Local tangent estimates can describe directions of neural or behavioral variation near a state. Their interpretation depends on neighborhood scale and should be compared with simpler local-linear null models.

Relevance to LDG

At L2, Tangent Space connects intrinsic curve/derivation definitions, coordinate representations, differentials, embedded subspaces, vector fields, and later Riemannian geometry without treating local linearization as global structure.

# Tangent Space

## One-sentence definition

## Why this concept matters

## Intuition

## Formal definition

Let $M$ be a smooth $n$-manifold and $p\in M$.

### Curve-velocity definition

Consider smooth curves $\gamma:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\to M$ with $\gamma(0)=p$. Two curves are tangent-equivalent at $p$ when, in one—and therefore every—chart $(U,x)$ containing $p$,

$$
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}x(\gamma_1(t))
=
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}x(\gamma_2(t)).
$$

A tangent vector is an equivalence class $[\gamma]$, and the tangent space $T_pM$ is the set of such classes with vector-space operations induced in coordinates. Mrowka gives the equivalent chart-representative construction [@Mrowka2004, sec. 7.2].

### Derivation definition

Equivalently, a tangent vector at $p$ is a linear map

$$
v:C^\infty_p(M)\to\mathbb R
$$

on germs of smooth functions satisfying the Leibniz rule

$$
v(fg)=f(p)v(g)+g(p)v(f).
$$

For a curve representative,

$$
v(f)
=
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}f(\gamma(t)).
$$

The curve-velocity and derivation descriptions define canonically isomorphic tangent spaces [@Lee1997, ch. 2]. This Concept states the equivalence but defers its proof.

### Coordinates and basis changes

A chart $x=(x^1,\ldots,x^n)$ supplies the coordinate basis

$$
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^1}\right|_p,
\ldots,
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^n}\right|_p,
$$

so every $v\in T_pM$ has components

$$
v
=
v^i\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p.
$$

Thus $\dim T_pM=n$. Under another chart $y=(y^1,\ldots,y^n)$,

$$
v'^a
=
\frac{\partial y^a}{\partial x^i}(p)v^i,
$$

and

$$
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p
=
\frac{\partial y^a}{\partial x^i}(p)
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial y^a}\right|_p.
$$

The components change, while the intrinsic tangent vector does not.

### Differential of a smooth map

For a smooth map $f:M\to N$, the differential at $p$ is the linear map

$$
df_p:T_pM\to T_{f(p)}N.
$$

Using curve representatives,

$$
df_p([\gamma])=[f\circ\gamma].
$$

Using derivations,

$$
(df_pv)(g)=v(g\circ f).
$$

In charts, the matrix of $df_p$ is the Jacobian $[\partial f^a/\partial x^i]_p$ [@Mrowka2004, sec. 7.2].

### Embedded tangent spaces

If $i:M\hookrightarrow\widetilde M$ is a smooth embedding, $di_p$ identifies $T_pM$ with a linear subspace of $T_{i(p)}\widetilde M$. For an embedded manifold in $\mathbb R^D$, this gives the familiar ambient tangent plane. This represented subspace depends on the embedding, whereas the abstract $T_pM$ is intrinsic. An arbitrary ambient subspace fitted to data is not automatically the image of an intrinsic tangent space.

### Tangent bundle and vector fields

The tangent bundle is the disjoint union

$$
TM=\bigsqcup_{p\in M}T_pM
$$

with its compatible smooth bundle structure. A smooth vector field is a smooth section $X:M\to TM$ satisfying $X(p)\in T_pM$. Bundle construction proofs and connection theory are deferred.

- $v(f)$: derivation of a smooth function germ.
