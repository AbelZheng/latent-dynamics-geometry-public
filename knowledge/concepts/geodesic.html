Geodesic

Geodesic

One-sentence definition

An affinely parameterized geodesic is a curve that follows the locally straight, zero-covariant-acceleration paths determined by a chosen connection, and for a Riemannian metric is locally length-minimizing over sufficiently short segments.

Why this concept matters

Geodesics generalize straight lines to curved spaces. They are often called shortest paths, but a geodesic need not minimize distance globally or beyond special local intervals.

Intuition

Great circles are geodesics on a sphere. A short arc is the shortest route between nearby points, while a sufficiently long arc may not be globally shortest.

Formal definition

Let \(M\) be a smooth manifold with a linear connection \(\nabla\), and let \(\gamma:I\to M\) be smooth. Its covariant acceleration is

\[ D_t\dot\gamma = \nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma. \]

An affinely parameterized geodesic satisfies

\[ D_t\dot\gamma=0. \]

In local coordinates \(x^1,\ldots,x^n\), this is the system

\[ \ddot x^k(t) + \Gamma^k_{ij}(x(t))\dot x^i(t)\dot x^j(t) =0, \]

where \(\Gamma^k_{ij}\) are the Christoffel symbols of the chosen connection in that chart (Lee 1997, ch. 4). They are coordinate coefficients, not tensor components by themselves; connection construction and transformation proofs are deferred.

Initial data and maximal geodesics

For \(p\in M\), \(v\in T_pM\), and initial time \(t_0\), the geodesic initial-value problem is

\[ \gamma(t_0)=p, \qquad \dot\gamma(t_0)=v. \]

Under the smooth-connection assumptions, these data determine a unique local geodesic and a unique maximal extension on an open interval containing \(t_0\) (Lee 1997, ch. 4). Maximal does not mean defined for all real time; that stronger property is geodesic completeness.

Affine reparameterization

If \(\gamma(t)\) is a geodesic and \(a\ne0\), then \(\widetilde\gamma(s)=\gamma(as+b)\) is again an affinely parameterized geodesic. A general nonlinear reparameterization usually adds acceleration parallel to the curve and does not satisfy the zero-acceleration equation, even though it traces the same path image. Unparameterized geodesic paths and affine geodesics must therefore be distinguished.

Riemannian specialization

For a Riemannian Metric, the relevant connection is the unique torsion-free, metric-compatible Levi–Civita connection. Its geodesics have constant speed because their velocity is parallel along the curve (Lee 1997, ch. 5). Every Riemannian geodesic is locally minimizing on sufficiently short intervals, and every length-minimizing smooth curve is a geodesic when given an affine (in particular, constant-speed) parametrization. These are local statements: a geodesic can cease to minimize after leaving a normal neighborhood, and multiple geodesics can join the same endpoints (Lee 1997, ch. 6).

Exponential map

Let \(\gamma_v\) denote the maximal geodesic with \(\gamma_v(0)=p\) and \(\dot\gamma_v(0)=v\). The exponential map is

\[ \exp_p(v)=\gamma_v(1) \]

for tangent vectors \(v\) whose geodesic is defined at time \(1\). Equivalently, \(\exp_p(tv)=\gamma_v(t)\) where defined. Near the zero vector, \(\exp_p\) provides normal coordinates and a local relation between tangent vectors and nearby points. It is not generally globally injective or defined on all of \(T_pM\) without completeness assumptions.

Core notation

  • \(\gamma:I\to M\): curve.
  • \(\dot\gamma\): velocity.
  • \(\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma\): covariant acceleration.
  • \(L(\gamma)\): Riemannian curve length.
  • \(\Gamma^k_{ij}\): Christoffel symbols of the chosen connection.
  • \(p,v\): initial point and tangent velocity.
  • \(\gamma_v\): maximal geodesic determined by \(v\in T_pM\).
  • \(\exp_p\): exponential map on its supported tangent-domain.

Prerequisites

Metric supplies general distance language. Manifold supplies the curved space on which the curve lies. The differential zero-acceleration definition additionally requires tangent and connection machinery; the local length-minimization statement requires a Riemannian metric and its Levi–Civita connection.

Assumed background: tangent vectors, covariant differentiation, affine parameterization, and the Levi–Civita connection; their theorem-level construction is deferred. The related Riemannian Metric object states the geometric structure but is later in the planning spine.

What this does NOT imply

A geodesic does not imply global shortestness, uniqueness between two points, geodesic completeness, global injectivity of the exponential map, a physical trajectory, constant coordinate velocity, Euclidean straightness, or reliable estimation from a nearest-neighbor graph.

Minimal example

On the unit sphere, great-circle arcs are geodesics. Between antipodal points there are many geodesics of equal length, demonstrating non-uniqueness.

Neuroscience / behavioral relevance

Geodesic distances are sometimes used for latent representations. Their validity depends on supported manifold and metric assumptions; graph approximations and visual curves are not sufficient evidence.

Relevance to LDG

At L2, Geodesic connects connection-based acceleration, coordinate equations, initial tangent data, the exponential map, and local Riemannian minimization while keeping global shortest paths and actual dynamical trajectories separate.
