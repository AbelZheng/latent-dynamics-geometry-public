Covariance-Prior Trajectories

Covariance-Prior Trajectories

This L1/stable Candidate F Toy Model compares static, two-stage, Gaussian-process, and first-order Markov priors for retrospective latent-path inference. It asks what is gained by a shared temporal covariance prior, and how that gain fails under incorrect kernels, observation laws, alignment, residual covariance, or missingness. Numerical values are deterministic outputs under master seed 20260819; they are conditional on this simulator, masks, plug-in parameters, and declared covariance families.

Assumed background: multivariate Gaussian conditioning, factor analysis, covariance kernels, Cholesky solves, linear-Gaussian smoothing, and independent train/validation/test trials.

Question

For multiple independent trials generated by two latent Gaussian processes with different timescales and observed through one known factor-analysis layer:

  1. does an exact known-parameter GPFA posterior recover latent paths and calibrated uncertainty;
  2. can a finite train-plus-validation timescale grid recover a useful plug-in prior without test or latent-truth leakage;
  3. how much do temporal coupling, cross-channel sharing, and the observation layer contribute to masked prediction;
  4. how does a one-lag-matched LGSSM/RTS approximation differ from the non-Markov squared-exponential prior; and
  5. how do changepoints, residual correlation, low counts, equal kernels, alignment error, informative missingness, and wrong covariance families break the interpretation?

A smooth posterior mean is a conditional expectation under a selected covariance prior and observation model. It is retrospective, parameter-conditional, and not a discovered transition equation, vector field, intrinsic trajectory, or mechanism (Yu et al. 2009; Cunningham and Yu 2014).

Ground truth

Dimensions, trials, and time grid

The latent dimension is \(q=2\), the observed dimension is \(p=6\), and every trial contains \(T=24\) samples on

\[ t_m=\frac{m}{23}, \qquad m=0,\ldots,23. \]

Whole trials are independent draws. Split sizes are:

Split Trials Use
train 8 fit learned baselines and score the finite timescale grid
validation 4 contribute to frozen grid selection and bounded baseline selection
test 5 untouched complete-trial and masked prediction evaluation

No path continuity, prior covariance, filtering state, or smoother state crosses a trial boundary.

Latent squared-exponential processes

For trial \(n\) and latent coordinate \(j\),

\[ x_j^{(n)}(\cdot)\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,k_j), \]

independently across \(j\) and \(n\). The true characteristic timescales are

\[ (\tau_1,\tau_2)=(0.08,0.30). \]

The latent nugget variance is fixed at

\[ \nu^2=0.02, \]

and marginal variance is fixed at one:

\[ k_j(t,t') = (1-\nu^2) \exp\left[-\frac{(t-t')^2}{2\tau_j^2}\right] +\nu^2\mathbf 1[t=t']. \]

Thus \(k_j(t,t)=1\). The smooth signal variance is \(0.98\) and the independent sampled-time latent nugget variance is \(0.02\). The nugget is not observation noise, LGSSM process noise, or parameter uncertainty. Fixing the latent variance removes loading/latent scale ambiguity while retaining sign and permutation ambiguity (Yu et al. 2009).

Every finite Gram matrix is assembled directly on the declared grid and sampled by a deterministic Cholesky factor. Latent draws and observation residuals use independent seed streams.

Observation layer

For each trial and time,

\[ y_t=Cx_t+d+\epsilon_t, \qquad \epsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(0,R), \]

with

\[ C= \begin{pmatrix} 1.00&0.25\\ 0.70&-0.45\\ 0.30&0.90\\ -0.65&0.40\\ 0.80&0.55\\ -0.25&-0.75 \end{pmatrix}, \]

\[ d=(0.30,-0.20,0.15,0.05,-0.10,0.25)^\top, \]

and

\[ R=\operatorname{diag}(0.10,0.16,0.22,0.30,0.38,0.48). \]

The implementation verifies that \(C\) has rank two, records its singular values and condition number, and retains the exact loading subspace \(\operatorname{col}(C)\). Ground-truth latent paths, kernels, and observation parameters enter fitting only for methods explicitly labelled known-parameter oracle.

Generative model

For each split and trial:

  1. independently draw each latent path from its finite-grid GP covariance;
  2. independently draw Gaussian observation residuals;
  3. generate the complete \(24\times6\) observation array;
  4. freeze the complete array and latent truth;
  5. create evaluation masks as separate views of selected test trials; and
  6. expose hidden observations and latent truth only to final scoring and implementation oracles.

The master seed is 20260819. Random streams use Generator(PCG64(SeedSequence(words))) with explicit stage, split, trial, latent, residual, mask, stress, and baseline codes stored in the manifest.

Generation, observation-layer fitting, timescale selection, posterior inference, masking, alignment, and scoring are separate functions. Parameter selection receives complete train and validation observations, but no test values, test masks, or latent arrays.

Parameters

Finite timescale grid

The known observation layer \(C,d,R\) is frozen. The selected-timescale GPFA baseline evaluates the nine ordered pairs

\[ \tau_1\in\{0.06,0.08,0.14\}, \qquad \tau_2\in\{0.18,0.30,0.45\}. \]

The true pair is included. For each pair, compute total train-plus-validation observation marginal negative log likelihood under independent trials. Select the minimum total NLL; break exact ties lexicographically by \((\tau_1,\tau_2)\). Test observations and every latent truth array are excluded.

This is a finite plug-in covariance-prior selection baseline. It is not unrestricted maximum-likelihood GPFA, does not learn \(C,d,R\), and does not integrate hyperparameter uncertainty. Rasmussen and Williams describe marginal-likelihood hyperparameter learning and its possible ridges or local alternatives; this finite grid deliberately avoids claiming a general optimizer (Rasmussen and Williams 2006, ch. 5).

Other fixed parameters

  • wrong common squared-exponential timescale: \((0.14,0.14)\);
  • static FA latent dimension: two;
  • six deterministic starts for bounded static FA;
  • FA uniqueness floor: \(10^{-6}\);
  • FA iteration cap: 300 and relative likelihood tolerance \(10^{-9}\);
  • covariance symmetry tolerance: \(10^{-10}\);
  • covariance PSD tolerance: \(10^{-9}\);
  • posterior/direct-conditioning discrepancy tolerance: \(10^{-9}\);
  • pointwise interval multiplier: 1.959963984540054; and
  • deterministic byte comparison for every artifact.

Simulation

Complete-trial evaluation

Before masking, score every test trial under each applicable marginal observation distribution. The same complete test realization may then be copied and masked for conditional prediction; complete values are never supplied to posterior conditioning in a masked evaluation.

Test masks

Use deterministic masks:

Test trial Mask Region label
1 channel 1 hidden at all 24 times masked_channel_1
2 channel 2 hidden at all 24 times masked_channel_2
3–5 all six channels hidden at time indices 8–14 inclusive masked_time_block

The contiguous time block contains seven bins. For a hidden channel, GPFA can borrow information through both temporal covariance and the other observed channels; an independent-channel GP cannot borrow cross-channel information. For a fully hidden time block, temporal priors can interpolate, while an independent-bin model reverts to its prior.

Expected behavior

  1. Correct known-parameter GPFA should match direct finite Gaussian conditioning and provide calibrated repeated-simulation uncertainty under the primary generator.
  2. A selected grid pair may equal or differ from the generating pair in finite samples. Either outcome is reported without calling the finite grid general parameter recovery.
  3. Static FA and independent-bin inference should recover instantaneous shared structure but cannot interpolate a fully missing time block from neighboring bins.
  4. Independent-channel GP smoothing can interpolate within an observed channel but cannot use other channels to reconstruct a channel hidden for the entire trial.
  5. GPFA can combine cross-channel loading information with temporal covariance for both masks.
  6. The AR(1)/RTS approximation matches one-lag correlation but not the full squared-exponential covariance, so longer-lag and masked-block predictions may differ even when one-step correlations agree.
  7. Wrong common timescale suppresses genuine timescale heterogeneity. Equal true kernels make coordinate-specific latent axes rotationally ambiguous if the observation layer is learned.
  8. Changepoints, correlated residuals, low-rate counts, alignment errors, and informative masks violate different model assumptions and must remain visible rather than tuned away.

Parameter manipulations

A. Correct distinct-timescale Gaussian condition

Use the primary generator, diagonal \(R\), complete alignment, ignorable masks, and the declared squared-exponential kernels. This is the only primary calibration condition.

B. Latent changepoint

Generate a primary latent trial and add

\[ x_1(t)\leftarrow x_1(t)+2\mathbf 1[t\ge0.5] \]

before applying the observation layer. The squared-exponential prior is misspecified at the abrupt step. Report complete and masked prediction, posterior smoothing across the discontinuity, residual structure, and interval coverage. A smooth bridge is prior-conditioned bias, not evidence that the underlying path was smooth.

C. Correlated observation residuals

Retain the marginal residual variances but use

\[ R_{ij}^{\mathrm{corr}} = \sqrt{R_{ii}R_{jj}}\,0.28^{|i-j|}. \]

The fitted GPFA and FA models still assume diagonal \(R\). Report held-out NLL, cross-channel residual covariance, loading-subspace distortion for learned baselines, latent recovery, and coverage. Do not report diagonal-uniqueness recovery as a valid target.

D. Low-rate Poisson counts

Set

\[ \lambda_{t,j} = \exp\left(operatorname{clip}([Cx_t+d]_j,-1.5,1.1)\right) \]

and draw

\[ n_{t,j}\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_{t,j}). \]

Analyze

\[ y_{t,j}^{\sqrt{\ }}=\sqrt{n_{t,j}+0.25} \]

with the bounded Gaussian methods. Rates range approximately from \(0.22\) to \(3.00\) expected counts per bin, so discreteness, skew, and variance dependence remain material. Gaussian NLL and intervals are explicitly labelled approximation diagnostics; no count-likelihood calibration or correction is claimed (Yu et al. 2009).

E. Equal latent kernels

Set

\[ \tau_1=\tau_2=0.18 \]

with the same unit marginal covariance convention. When \(C\) and kernels are jointly learned, any orthogonal rotation of the two latent coordinates with a corresponding loading rotation preserves the model family. Therefore:

  • known-\(C\) oracle coordinates remain fixed only by externally supplied \(C\);
  • learned FA/two-stage/GPFA representatives are scored by the joint two-dimensional loading subspace and optimally aligned path subspace;
  • coordinate-specific timescale or sign/permutation claims are omitted.

A Procrustes rotation may be estimated from training truth for simulation scoring, frozen, and applied to test paths. It is never used in fitting or parameter selection.

F. Trial alignment jitter

For each trial, draw an offset

\[ \delta_n\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\Delta t. \]

Generate each latent GP on an extended grid and observe \(x_n(t_m+\delta_n)\), while the analysis labels samples by nominal \(t_m\). Score recovery against the nominal event-relative latent path \(x_n(t_m)\). Because the primary covariance is stationary, a whole-trial shift can leave the marginal observation likelihood nearly unchanged even while event-relative path recovery degrades. This condition therefore tests alignment semantics, not merely covariance fit. No circular wrapping is allowed.

G. Informative missingness

Hide an observation whenever

\[ |y_{t,j}|\ge1.25. \]

The ordinary row-selection posterior deliberately ignores information carried by the mask. Report missing fraction, latent/observation magnitude inside and outside the mask, and uncorrected posterior RMSE. Coverage and predictive NLL are labelled warning-only under informative missingness and excluded from acceptance. No missingness correction is fabricated.

H. Wrong common-timescale squared-exponential prior

Use

\[ (\tau_1,\tau_2)=(0.14,0.14) \]

with known \(C,d,R\). This isolates the cost of incorrectly forcing one common smoothness scale while retaining the same covariance family.

I. Wrong covariance family: one-lag-matched AR(1)

For each latent coordinate, define

\[ \rho_j =\frac{k_j(t_m,t_{m+1})}{k_j(t_m,t_m)} =(1-\nu^2) \exp\left[-\frac{\Delta t^2}{2\tau_j^2}\right]. \]

Use a stationary scalar AR(1)

\[ x_{t+1,j}=\rho_jx_{t,j}+w_{t,j}, \qquad w_{t,j}\sim\mathcal N(0,1-\rho_j^2), \]

with the same \(C,d,R\). This matches marginal variance and one-lag covariance but has exponential rather than squared-exponential longer-lag decay. Infer paths with Kalman filtering plus fixed-interval RTS smoothing and compare with direct batch conditioning under the AR covariance (Yu et al. 2009).

Sanity checks

The verifier must assert:

  1. Kernel convention: every primary latent Gram matrix has diagonal one, smooth variance \(0.98\), nugget variance \(0.02\), and the declared timescale.
  2. Covariance PSD: all latent, observation, masked-observation, posterior, static-FA, and AR covariances are symmetric within \(10^{-10}\) and have minimum eigenvalue at least \(-10^{-9}\).
  3. Generator algebra: noiseless observations equal \(Cx+d\) exactly within floating-point tolerance.
  4. Split/reset integrity: trials regenerate independently, identifiers are disjoint, and no covariance or posterior block connects different trials.
  5. Mask exclusion: hidden values are absent from conditioning matrices, likelihood contributions, preprocessing, and parameter selection.
  6. No latent leakage: replacing every latent-truth array leaves grid scores, learned baselines, posterior distributions, and observation predictions unchanged.
  7. No test leakage: poisoning all test observations leaves \(C,d,R\) oracle declarations, learned training baselines, grid scores, selected tau, and validation records unchanged.
  8. Grid recomputation: every train and validation marginal NLL is independently recomputed from the declared covariance and the selected pair follows the exact tie rule.
  9. Direct posterior equivalence: covariance-form, precision-form, and generic joint-Gaussian conditioning means/covariances agree within \(10^{-9}\) for complete and masked short trials.
  10. Predictive conditioning equivalence: hidden-observation conditional means/covariances agree with the corresponding blocks of direct joint conditioning.
  11. RTS/batch equivalence: AR(1) Kalman/RTS means, marginal covariances, and adjacent cross-covariances agree with direct AR batch conditioning within \(10^{-9}\).
  12. Static independent-bin limit: setting temporal covariance to identity yields per-time posterior results equal to separate Gaussian conditioning at each bin.
  13. Loading applicability: loading-subspace errors are emitted only for learned static/two-stage methods; known-\(C\) methods are labelled oracle rather than assigned trivial recovery credit.
  14. Alignment scope: sign/permutation or Procrustes transformations are fit using training truth for scoring only, frozen before test scoring, and never affect likelihood or posterior computation.
  15. Equal-kernel guard: coordinate-specific path/tau rows are absent for learned equal-kernel models; joint-subspace metrics remain.
  16. Posterior nesting: masking cannot reduce posterior variance relative to conditioning on the corresponding complete observation set, within numerical tolerance.
  17. Coverage provenance: every coverage row identifies latent versus observation target, complete versus masked region, condition, and fixed parameter source.
  18. Determinism: a clean temporary run produces byte-identical manifest.json, metrics.csv, diagnostics.json, and summary.png.
  19. Metric schema: all keys are unique, labels are nonempty, values are finite, and inapplicable metrics are omitted rather than zero-filled.

These checks verify finite Gaussian calculations and leakage boundaries, not real-data model adequacy.

Failure cases

  • Nugget added on top of unit signal variance: this silently changes \(k(t,t)\) from one and breaks the declared scale convention.
  • Bins split across parameter-learning folds: time bins from one trial are dependent and cannot serve as independent train/test units.
  • Masked targets assimilated before scoring: reconstruction of already observed values is not conditional prediction.
  • Grid called general maximum likelihood: nine fixed candidates with known \(C,d,R\) are not unrestricted GPFA fitting.
  • Oracle and selected models merged: known true tau and train/validation-selected tau answer different questions.
  • Posterior covariance called total uncertainty: it omits uncertainty in selected timescales and learned observation parameters.
  • Static FA called temporal inference: per-bin posterior calculations do not couple neighboring times.
  • Two-stage smoothing called GPFA: smoothing observations before static dimensionality reduction solves a different objective.
  • Independent-channel smoothing credited with cross-channel prediction: a fully hidden channel receives no information from other channels under that baseline.
  • RTS approximation called the same prior: one-lag agreement does not reproduce squared-exponential higher-lag covariance.
  • Equal-kernel coordinates scored without rotation allowance: axes within the equal-kernel latent subspace are not uniquely identified when the loading layer is learned.
  • Poisson square-root scores called calibrated Gaussian probabilities: low counts remain discrete and heteroscedastic.
  • Correlated residuals forced into factors: apparent shared trajectories may absorb observation-model misspecification.
  • Informative masks treated as ignorable: the missingness pattern itself carries information.
  • Alignment jitter ignored: a stationary marginal likelihood can look adequate while event-relative path interpretation is wrong.
  • Smooth posterior mean called dynamics: covariance-based interpolation is not a learned transition law or mechanism.

Recovery task

Complete finite GPFA distribution

For one trial, stack time-major latent and observation vectors. Let

\[ K(\tau_1,\tau_2)\in\mathbb R^{48\times48} \]

contain the two independent coordinate kernels, and define

\[ \bar C=I_{24}\otimes C, \quad \bar d=\mathbf1_{24}\otimes d, \quad \bar R=I_{24}\otimes R. \]

Then

\[ \mathbf y\sim\mathcal N \left(\bar d,\bar C K\bar C^\top+\bar R\right). \]

Marginal likelihoods use Cholesky factors and triangular solves, never explicit determinants or inverses.

Full-interval latent posterior

For observed-entry selector \(H\),

\[ \mathbf y_O=H\mathbf y, \]

and

\[ \mu_{x\mid O} =K\bar C_O^\top (\bar C_OK\bar C_O^\top+\bar R_O)^{-1} (\mathbf y_O-\bar d_O), \]

\[ \Sigma_{x\mid O} =K-K\bar C_O^\top (\bar C_OK\bar C_O^\top+\bar R_O)^{-1} \bar C_OK. \]

This uses the complete retained trial on both sides of a missing block and is retrospective. It is not online filtering.

Hidden-observation prediction

Partition observed and hidden entries of the marginal Gaussian observation law and compute

\[ p(\mathbf y_H\mid\mathbf y_O,\theta). \]

Score only hidden values. Report joint predictive NLL, per-entry RMSE, marginal 95% coverage, and average predictive variance. For a whole hidden channel, preserve cross-time covariance in the joint predictive density rather than summing univariate marginals.

Static FA and independent-bin posterior

Fit bounded two-factor diagonal-noise FA to pooled training time bins, treating bins as independent for fitting. Use six deterministic starts; choose maximal training likelihood, then lower validation NLL and start identifier for ties. Infer each test time independently using the fitted Gaussian factor posterior. This provides no interpolation across a fully hidden time block.

Also compute a known-layer independent-bin oracle using true \(C,d,R\) and \(x_t\sim\mathcal N(0,I_2)\) independently over time. This isolates temporal-prior effects without conflating observation-layer estimation.

Mean plus diagonal Gaussian

Fit one training mean and variance per observed channel, independent across time and channels. It has no latent path. It receives complete and masked observation NLL/RMSE/coverage but no latent recovery or loading-subspace row.

Independent-channel GP smoother

For channel \(i\), use the known marginal covariance

\[ K_i^y =\sum_{j=1}^2C_{ij}^2K_j+R_{ii}I. \]

Condition each channel separately on its observed times. This is explicitly labelled a known-marginal independent-channel oracle: it uses true channel covariance but no cross-channel information. It supplies a controlled upper reference for separate smoothing, not a learned real-data method.

Smoothing then PCA/FA

Apply the independent-channel smoother to training observations, then fit rank-two PCA and bounded diagonal FA to the smoothed pooled bins. Freeze loadings and apply the same smoother/maps to validation and test trials. Score loading subspace, aligned latent paths, combined observation reconstruction, and masked predictions. These two-stage procedures receive their marginal target through a separate smoother and are not equivalent to joint GPFA.

Known and selected GPFA

  • Known-parameter GPFA oracle: true \(C,d,R,\tau_1,\tau_2,\nu^2\).
  • Selected-tau GPFA: true \(C,d,R,\nu^2\) plus the frozen train/validation-selected tau pair.
  • Wrong-common-tau GPFA: true \(C,d,R,\nu^2\) plus \((0.14,0.14)\).

All return exact plug-in path posteriors. Only the first is a true-parameter oracle.

LGSSM/RTS approximation

Use the one-lag-matched AR coefficients and process variances above with stationary initial covariance \(I_2\) and true \(C,d,R\). Run independent Kalman filters and fixed-interval RTS smoothers per trial, respecting masks and resets. The smoother is a retrospective inference algorithm for the AR approximation, not a discovered state transition.

Candidate analysis methods

  1. Training mean plus diagonal Gaussian. Observation-only independent reference.
  2. Bounded learned static FA. Pooled-bin loading baseline and independent-bin posterior.
  3. Known-layer independent-bin Gaussian posterior. Oracle observation layer without temporal coupling.
  4. Known-marginal independent-channel GP smoothing. Temporal but no cross-channel sharing.
  5. Smoothing-then-PCA. Two-stage learned loading subspace.
  6. Smoothing-then-FA. Two-stage probabilistic loading baseline.
  7. Known-parameter exact GPFA oracle. Correct primary model.
  8. Finite-grid selected-tau GPFA. Known layer, selected covariance prior.
  9. Wrong-common-timescale GPFA. Misspecified smoothness baseline.
  10. One-lag-matched LGSSM/Kalman/RTS. Wrong-family Markov approximation.
  11. Structured negative-condition evaluations. Applicability-limited diagnostics, not new winning methods.

Evaluation

Loading-subspace recovery

For learned loading basis \(\widehat C\) and true \(C\), report principal angles, maximum angle, RMS sine error, and

\[ \lVert P_{\widehat C}-P_C\rVert_F. \]

Only static FA/PCA and smoothing-then-FA/PCA receive these rows. Known-layer methods are labelled oracle and do not receive zero-valued “recovery” metrics.

Latent path alignment

Known-layer GPFA with distinct coordinate-specific kernels retains only the declared sign/permutation conventions. Static PCA/FA and two-stage PCA/FA do not use those distinct temporal kernels and retain a broader orthogonal coordinate ambiguity. For each learned static/two-stage baseline, determine one full orthogonal Procrustes alignment from pooled training estimates and training latent truth, freeze it, transform posterior covariance consistently where coverage is reported, and apply it unchanged to validation/test paths. Do not optimize alignment separately per test trial.

For equal kernels, use one training-fitted orthogonal Procrustes transformation for the joint two-dimensional path and report joint-subspace/path error. Coordinate-specific tau or path claims are not applicable.

Posterior latent metrics

Report:

  • posterior-mean RMSE;
  • pointwise 95% marginal coverage;
  • mean posterior variance;
  • path roughness \(T^{-1}\sum_t\lVert\mu_{t+1}-\mu_t\rVert^2\); and
  • for temporally masked GPFA/AR posterior paths, block-time versus outside-block RMSE/coverage where emitted.

Full-path Mahalanobis and simultaneous-band diagnostics are useful extensions but are outside this bounded acceptance record; pointwise coverage and covariance-form oracles are retained here.

Coverage is repeated-simulation behavior conditional on fixed parameters. Selected-tau intervals omit timescale-selection uncertainty; learned baseline intervals omit loading-parameter uncertainty.

Complete held-out trial NLL

Report marginal NLL per observed entry on untouched complete test trials for every explicit Gaussian observation model. Static independent-bin methods factor over times; GPFA and AR models use joint within-trial covariance. Poisson stress rows are labelled Gaussian approximation scores.

Masked observation prediction

For each mask region, report joint predictive NLL per hidden entry, RMSE, and marginal 95% coverage for coherent probabilistic prediction methods. Mean predictive variance and hidden count are additionally required for the independent-channel baseline, where they make its fully hidden-channel prior behavior explicit. Separate complete-channel from contiguous-time-block results. Deterministic two-stage PCA/FA reconstructions report hidden-target RMSE only and do not fabricate raw-scale likelihood or coverage.

Timescale recovery

For the selected grid pair, report selected values, absolute/relative errors, grid rank, train NLL, validation NLL, combined score, and gap to the next candidate. No coordinate-specific timescale recovery is reported in the equal-kernel learned condition.

Residual diagnostics

For applicable Gaussian models, report residual mean and marginal variance, lag-1 autocorrelation, cross-channel residual covariance and maximum off-diagonal magnitude, plus explicit around/outside-changepoint summaries. Cholesky-whitened tails and residual Mahalanobis summaries are outside this bounded acceptance record and remain future extensions.

Residual whiteness under one ordering is a model diagnostic, not proof of the latent mechanism.

Tidy metrics schema

Every metrics.csv row contains exactly:

split,condition,method,target,region,metric,value

All values are finite. Inapplicable metrics are omitted and explained in diagnostics.json; warning-only rows include their applicability in method or target labels.

Interpretation

The correct-model oracle tests finite Gaussian inference, not model discovery. The selected-tau baseline tests a tiny predeclared covariance-prior choice while holding the observation layer known. Learned static/two-stage baselines test loading-subspace estimation separately. RTS tests a first-order Markov covariance approximation, not the same squared-exponential prior.

A posterior mean can be smoother than the realized latent path because it averages under a smooth prior. That is expected Bayesian conditioning, not evidence that the method uncovered a transition law. A credible interval conditional on one selected tau pair is not total uncertainty. Better held-out prediction supports one bounded observation/prior combination and does not identify mechanism, dynamics, or unique latent coordinates.

TipLDG synthesis

GPFA specifies covariance among latent values over time; LGSSM specifies a Markov transition and process noise. Both can yield retrospective Gaussian smoothers, but similar posterior plots do not make their model assumptions interchangeable.

What this toy model does NOT establish

  • It does not discover a transition matrix, vector field, flow, or differential equation from the GP covariance.
  • It does not make smooth posterior means measured latent trajectories.
  • It does not make fixed-interval posterior estimates online or causal.
  • It does not include parameter or model-selection uncertainty in plug-in intervals.
  • It does not prove a unique loading basis or latent coordinate system.
  • It does not identify coordinate-specific paths under equal kernels without an external convention.
  • It does not validate diagonal residuals, Gaussian transformed counts, stationary kernels, or ignorable missingness outside tested conditions.
  • It does not turn timescale recovery into a biological relaxation constant or mechanism.
  • It does not infer intrinsic dimension, manifold structure, topology, or nonlinear geometry.
  • It does not claim universal superiority of GPFA over FA, smoothing, or LGSSM.
  • It does not call a finite grid unrestricted maximum likelihood.

Extensions to real data

A real-data use must justify trial boundaries, simultaneity, binning, count transformation, offsets, loading dimension, residual covariance, kernel family, alignment, missingness, parameter sharing, and train/validation/test units. Low counts may require a count likelihood. Correlated residuals may require a richer observation model. Alignment uncertainty, nonstationarity, session drift, and condition changes need explicit models or sensitivity analyses.

Model selection should be nested; posterior calibration should use held-out channels/times/trials; and external behavior should be tested downstream without choosing latent representations on the final test set. Smooth trajectories should be presented with uncertainty and prior sensitivity, not as direct evidence of neural dynamics or mechanism.

# Covariance-Prior Trajectories

This L1/stable Candidate F Toy Model compares static, two-stage, Gaussian-process, and first-order Markov priors for retrospective latent-path inference. It asks what is gained by a shared temporal covariance prior, and how that gain fails under incorrect kernels, observation laws, alignment, residual covariance, or missingness. Numerical values are deterministic outputs under master seed `20260819`; they are conditional on this simulator, masks, plug-in parameters, and declared covariance families.

**Assumed background:** multivariate Gaussian conditioning, factor analysis, covariance kernels, Cholesky solves, linear-Gaussian smoothing, and independent train/validation/test trials.

## Question

For multiple independent trials generated by two latent Gaussian processes with different timescales and observed through one known factor-analysis layer:

1. does an exact known-parameter GPFA posterior recover latent paths and calibrated uncertainty;
2. can a finite train-plus-validation timescale grid recover a useful plug-in prior without test or latent-truth leakage;
3. how much do temporal coupling, cross-channel sharing, and the observation layer contribute to masked prediction;
4. how does a one-lag-matched LGSSM/RTS approximation differ from the non-Markov squared-exponential prior; and
5. how do changepoints, residual correlation, low counts, equal kernels, alignment error, informative missingness, and wrong covariance families break the interpretation?

A smooth posterior mean is a conditional expectation under a selected covariance prior and observation model. It is retrospective, parameter-conditional, and not a discovered transition equation, vector field, intrinsic trajectory, or mechanism [@YuEtAl2009; @CunninghamYu2014].

## Ground truth

### Dimensions, trials, and time grid

The latent dimension is $q=2$, the observed dimension is $p=6$, and every trial contains $T=24$ samples on

$$
t_m=\frac{m}{23},
\qquad m=0,\ldots,23.
$$

Whole trials are independent draws. Split sizes are:

| Split | Trials | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| train | 8 | fit learned baselines and score the finite timescale grid |
| validation | 4 | contribute to frozen grid selection and bounded baseline selection |
| test | 5 | untouched complete-trial and masked prediction evaluation |

No path continuity, prior covariance, filtering state, or smoother state crosses a trial boundary.

### Latent squared-exponential processes

For trial $n$ and latent coordinate $j$,

$$
x_j^{(n)}(\cdot)\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,k_j),
$$

independently across $j$ and $n$. The true characteristic timescales are

$$
(\tau_1,\tau_2)=(0.08,0.30).
$$

The latent nugget variance is fixed at

$$
\nu^2=0.02,
$$

and marginal variance is fixed at one:

$$
k_j(t,t')
=
(1-\nu^2)
\exp\left[-\frac{(t-t')^2}{2\tau_j^2}\right]
+\nu^2\mathbf 1[t=t'].
$$

Thus $k_j(t,t)=1$. The smooth signal variance is $0.98$ and the independent sampled-time latent nugget variance is $0.02$. The nugget is not observation noise, LGSSM process noise, or parameter uncertainty. Fixing the latent variance removes loading/latent scale ambiguity while retaining sign and permutation ambiguity [@YuEtAl2009].

Every finite Gram matrix is assembled directly on the declared grid and sampled by a deterministic Cholesky factor. Latent draws and observation residuals use independent seed streams.

### Observation layer

For each trial and time,

$$
y_t=Cx_t+d+\epsilon_t,
\qquad
\epsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(0,R),
$$

with

$$
C=
\begin{pmatrix}
1.00&0.25\\
0.70&-0.45\\
0.30&0.90\\
-0.65&0.40\\
0.80&0.55\\
-0.25&-0.75
\end{pmatrix},
$$

$$
d=(0.30,-0.20,0.15,0.05,-0.10,0.25)^\top,
$$

and

$$
R=\operatorname{diag}(0.10,0.16,0.22,0.30,0.38,0.48).
$$

The implementation verifies that $C$ has rank two, records its singular values and condition number, and retains the exact loading subspace $\operatorname{col}(C)$. Ground-truth latent paths, kernels, and observation parameters enter fitting only for methods explicitly labelled **known-parameter oracle**.

## Generative model

For each split and trial:

1. independently draw each latent path from its finite-grid GP covariance;
2. independently draw Gaussian observation residuals;
3. generate the complete $24\times6$ observation array;
4. freeze the complete array and latent truth;
5. create evaluation masks as separate views of selected test trials; and
6. expose hidden observations and latent truth only to final scoring and implementation oracles.

The master seed is `20260819`. Random streams use `Generator(PCG64(SeedSequence(words)))` with explicit stage, split, trial, latent, residual, mask, stress, and baseline codes stored in the manifest.

Generation, observation-layer fitting, timescale selection, posterior inference, masking, alignment, and scoring are separate functions. Parameter selection receives complete train and validation observations, but no test values, test masks, or latent arrays.

## Parameters

### Finite timescale grid

The known observation layer $C,d,R$ is frozen. The selected-timescale GPFA baseline evaluates the nine ordered pairs

$$
\tau_1\in\{0.06,0.08,0.14\},
\qquad
\tau_2\in\{0.18,0.30,0.45\}.
$$

The true pair is included. For each pair, compute total train-plus-validation observation marginal negative log likelihood under independent trials. Select the minimum total NLL; break exact ties lexicographically by $(\tau_1,\tau_2)$. Test observations and every latent truth array are excluded.

This is a finite plug-in covariance-prior selection baseline. It is not unrestricted maximum-likelihood GPFA, does not learn $C,d,R$, and does not integrate hyperparameter uncertainty. Rasmussen and Williams describe marginal-likelihood hyperparameter learning and its possible ridges or local alternatives; this finite grid deliberately avoids claiming a general optimizer [@RasmussenWilliams2006, ch. 5].

## Simulation

### Complete-trial evaluation

Before masking, score every test trial under each applicable marginal observation distribution. The same complete test realization may then be copied and masked for conditional prediction; complete values are never supplied to posterior conditioning in a masked evaluation.

### Test masks

Use deterministic masks:

| Test trial | Mask | Region label |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | channel 1 hidden at all 24 times | `masked_channel_1` |
| 2 | channel 2 hidden at all 24 times | `masked_channel_2` |
| 3--5 | all six channels hidden at time indices 8--14 inclusive | `masked_time_block` |

The contiguous time block contains seven bins. For a hidden channel, GPFA can borrow information through both temporal covariance and the other observed channels; an independent-channel GP cannot borrow cross-channel information. For a fully hidden time block, temporal priors can interpolate, while an independent-bin model reverts to its prior.

## Expected behavior

1. Correct known-parameter GPFA should match direct finite Gaussian conditioning and provide calibrated repeated-simulation uncertainty under the primary generator.
2. A selected grid pair may equal or differ from the generating pair in finite samples. Either outcome is reported without calling the finite grid general parameter recovery.
3. Static FA and independent-bin inference should recover instantaneous shared structure but cannot interpolate a fully missing time block from neighboring bins.
4. Independent-channel GP smoothing can interpolate within an observed channel but cannot use other channels to reconstruct a channel hidden for the entire trial.
5. GPFA can combine cross-channel loading information with temporal covariance for both masks.

## Parameter manipulations

### A. Correct distinct-timescale Gaussian condition

Use the primary generator, diagonal $R$, complete alignment, ignorable masks, and the declared squared-exponential kernels. This is the only primary calibration condition.

### B. Latent changepoint

Generate a primary latent trial and add

$$
x_1(t)\leftarrow x_1(t)+2\mathbf 1[t\ge0.5]
$$

before applying the observation layer. The squared-exponential prior is misspecified at the abrupt step. Report complete and masked prediction, posterior smoothing across the discontinuity, residual structure, and interval coverage. A smooth bridge is prior-conditioned bias, not evidence that the underlying path was smooth.

### C. Correlated observation residuals

Retain the marginal residual variances but use

$$
R_{ij}^{\mathrm{corr}}
=
\sqrt{R_{ii}R_{jj}}\,0.28^{|i-j|}.
$$

The fitted GPFA and FA models still assume diagonal $R$. Report held-out NLL, cross-channel residual covariance, loading-subspace distortion for learned baselines, latent recovery, and coverage. Do not report diagonal-uniqueness recovery as a valid target.

### D. Low-rate Poisson counts

Set

$$
\lambda_{t,j}
=
\exp\left(operatorname{clip}([Cx_t+d]_j,-1.5,1.1)\right)
$$

and draw

$$
n_{t,j}\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_{t,j}).
$$

Analyze

$$
y_{t,j}^{\sqrt{\ }}=\sqrt{n_{t,j}+0.25}
$$

with the bounded Gaussian methods. Rates range approximately from $0.22$ to $3.00$ expected counts per bin, so discreteness, skew, and variance dependence remain material. Gaussian NLL and intervals are explicitly labelled approximation diagnostics; no count-likelihood calibration or correction is claimed [@YuEtAl2009].

### E. Equal latent kernels

Set

$$
\tau_1=\tau_2=0.18
$$

with the same unit marginal covariance convention. When $C$ and kernels are jointly learned, any orthogonal rotation of the two latent coordinates with a corresponding loading rotation preserves the model family. Therefore:

- known-$C$ oracle coordinates remain fixed only by externally supplied $C$;
- learned FA/two-stage/GPFA representatives are scored by the joint two-dimensional loading subspace and optimally aligned path subspace;
- coordinate-specific timescale or sign/permutation claims are omitted.

A Procrustes rotation may be estimated from training truth for simulation scoring, frozen, and applied to test paths. It is never used in fitting or parameter selection.

### F. Trial alignment jitter

For each trial, draw an offset

$$
\delta_n\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\Delta t.
$$

Generate each latent GP on an extended grid and observe $x_n(t_m+\delta_n)$, while the analysis labels samples by nominal $t_m$. Score recovery against the nominal event-relative latent path $x_n(t_m)$. Because the primary covariance is stationary, a whole-trial shift can leave the marginal observation likelihood nearly unchanged even while event-relative path recovery degrades. This condition therefore tests alignment semantics, not merely covariance fit. No circular wrapping is allowed.

### G. Informative missingness

Hide an observation whenever

$$
|y_{t,j}|\ge1.25.
$$

The ordinary row-selection posterior deliberately ignores information carried by the mask. Report missing fraction, latent/observation magnitude inside and outside the mask, and uncorrected posterior RMSE. Coverage and predictive NLL are labelled **warning-only under informative missingness** and excluded from acceptance. No missingness correction is fabricated.

### H. Wrong common-timescale squared-exponential prior

Use

$$
(\tau_1,\tau_2)=(0.14,0.14)
$$

with known $C,d,R$. This isolates the cost of incorrectly forcing one common smoothness scale while retaining the same covariance family.

### I. Wrong covariance family: one-lag-matched AR(1)

For each latent coordinate, define

$$
\rho_j
=\frac{k_j(t_m,t_{m+1})}{k_j(t_m,t_m)}
=(1-\nu^2)
\exp\left[-\frac{\Delta t^2}{2\tau_j^2}\right].
$$

Use a stationary scalar AR(1)

$$
x_{t+1,j}=\rho_jx_{t,j}+w_{t,j},
\qquad
w_{t,j}\sim\mathcal N(0,1-\rho_j^2),
$$

with the same $C,d,R$. This matches marginal variance and one-lag covariance but has exponential rather than squared-exponential longer-lag decay. Infer paths with Kalman filtering plus fixed-interval RTS smoothing and compare with direct batch conditioning under the AR covariance [@YuEtAl2009].

## Failure cases

- **Nugget added on top of unit signal variance:** this silently changes $k(t,t)$ from one and breaks the declared scale convention.
- **Bins split across parameter-learning folds:** time bins from one trial are dependent and cannot serve as independent train/test units.
- **Masked targets assimilated before scoring:** reconstruction of already observed values is not conditional prediction.
- **Grid called general maximum likelihood:** nine fixed candidates with known $C,d,R$ are not unrestricted GPFA fitting.
- **Oracle and selected models merged:** known true tau and train/validation-selected tau answer different questions.
- **Posterior covariance called total uncertainty:** it omits uncertainty in selected timescales and learned observation parameters.
- **Static FA called temporal inference:** per-bin posterior calculations do not couple neighboring times.
- **Two-stage smoothing called GPFA:** smoothing observations before static dimensionality reduction solves a different objective.
- **Independent-channel smoothing credited with cross-channel prediction:** a fully hidden channel receives no information from other channels under that baseline.
- **RTS approximation called the same prior:** one-lag agreement does not reproduce squared-exponential higher-lag covariance.
- **Equal-kernel coordinates scored without rotation allowance:** axes within the equal-kernel latent subspace are not uniquely identified when the loading layer is learned.
- **Poisson square-root scores called calibrated Gaussian probabilities:** low counts remain discrete and heteroscedastic.
- **Correlated residuals forced into factors:** apparent shared trajectories may absorb observation-model misspecification.
- **Informative masks treated as ignorable:** the missingness pattern itself carries information.
- **Alignment jitter ignored:** a stationary marginal likelihood can look adequate while event-relative path interpretation is wrong.
- **Smooth posterior mean called dynamics:** covariance-based interpolation is not a learned transition law or mechanism.

## Recovery task

### Complete finite GPFA distribution

For one trial, stack time-major latent and observation vectors. Let

$$
K(\tau_1,\tau_2)\in\mathbb R^{48\times48}
$$

contain the two independent coordinate kernels, and define

$$
\bar C=I_{24}\otimes C,
\quad
\bar d=\mathbf1_{24}\otimes d,
\quad
\bar R=I_{24}\otimes R.
$$

Then

$$
\mathbf y\sim\mathcal N
\left(\bar d,\bar C K\bar C^\top+\bar R\right).
$$

Marginal likelihoods use Cholesky factors and triangular solves, never explicit determinants or inverses.

### Full-interval latent posterior

For observed-entry selector $H$,

$$
\mathbf y_O=H\mathbf y,
$$

and

$$
\mu_{x\mid O}
=K\bar C_O^\top
(\bar C_OK\bar C_O^\top+\bar R_O)^{-1}
(\mathbf y_O-\bar d_O),
$$

$$
\Sigma_{x\mid O}
=K-K\bar C_O^\top
(\bar C_OK\bar C_O^\top+\bar R_O)^{-1}
\bar C_OK.
$$

This uses the complete retained trial on both sides of a missing block and is retrospective. It is not online filtering.

### Hidden-observation prediction

Partition observed and hidden entries of the marginal Gaussian observation law and compute

$$
p(\mathbf y_H\mid\mathbf y_O,\theta).
$$

Score only hidden values. Report joint predictive NLL, per-entry RMSE, marginal 95% coverage, and average predictive variance. For a whole hidden channel, preserve cross-time covariance in the joint predictive density rather than summing univariate marginals.

### Static FA and independent-bin posterior

Fit bounded two-factor diagonal-noise FA to pooled training time bins, treating bins as independent for fitting. Use six deterministic starts; choose maximal training likelihood, then lower validation NLL and start identifier for ties. Infer each test time independently using the fitted Gaussian factor posterior. This provides no interpolation across a fully hidden time block.

Also compute a **known-layer independent-bin oracle** using true $C,d,R$ and $x_t\sim\mathcal N(0,I_2)$ independently over time. This isolates temporal-prior effects without conflating observation-layer estimation.

### Mean plus diagonal Gaussian

Fit one training mean and variance per observed channel, independent across time and channels. It has no latent path. It receives complete and masked observation NLL/RMSE/coverage but no latent recovery or loading-subspace row.

### Independent-channel GP smoother

For channel $i$, use the known marginal covariance

$$
K_i^y
=\sum_{j=1}^2C_{ij}^2K_j+R_{ii}I.
$$

Condition each channel separately on its observed times. This is explicitly labelled a **known-marginal independent-channel oracle**: it uses true channel covariance but no cross-channel information. It supplies a controlled upper reference for separate smoothing, not a learned real-data method.

### Smoothing then PCA/FA

Apply the independent-channel smoother to training observations, then fit rank-two PCA and bounded diagonal FA to the smoothed pooled bins. Freeze loadings and apply the same smoother/maps to validation and test trials. Score loading subspace, aligned latent paths, combined observation reconstruction, and masked predictions. These two-stage procedures receive their marginal target through a separate smoother and are not equivalent to joint GPFA.

### Known and selected GPFA

- **Known-parameter GPFA oracle:** true $C,d,R,\tau_1,\tau_2,\nu^2$.
- **Selected-tau GPFA:** true $C,d,R,\nu^2$ plus the frozen train/validation-selected tau pair.
- **Wrong-common-tau GPFA:** true $C,d,R,\nu^2$ plus $(0.14,0.14)$.

All return exact plug-in path posteriors. Only the first is a true-parameter oracle.

### LGSSM/RTS approximation

Use the one-lag-matched AR coefficients and process variances above with stationary initial covariance $I_2$ and true $C,d,R$. Run independent Kalman filters and fixed-interval RTS smoothers per trial, respecting masks and resets. The smoother is a retrospective inference algorithm for the AR approximation, not a discovered state transition.

## Evaluation

### Loading-subspace recovery

For learned loading basis $\widehat C$ and true $C$, report principal angles, maximum angle, RMS sine error, and

$$
\lVert P_{\widehat C}-P_C\rVert_F.
$$

Only static FA/PCA and smoothing-then-FA/PCA receive these rows. Known-layer methods are labelled oracle and do not receive zero-valued “recovery” metrics.

### Latent path alignment

Known-layer GPFA with distinct coordinate-specific kernels retains only the declared sign/permutation conventions. Static PCA/FA and two-stage PCA/FA do not use those distinct temporal kernels and retain a broader orthogonal coordinate ambiguity. For each learned static/two-stage baseline, determine one full orthogonal Procrustes alignment from pooled training estimates and training latent truth, freeze it, transform posterior covariance consistently where coverage is reported, and apply it unchanged to validation/test paths. Do not optimize alignment separately per test trial.

For equal kernels, use one training-fitted orthogonal Procrustes transformation for the joint two-dimensional path and report joint-subspace/path error. Coordinate-specific tau or path claims are not applicable.

### Posterior latent metrics

Report:

- posterior-mean RMSE;
- pointwise 95% marginal coverage;
- mean posterior variance;
- path roughness $T^{-1}\sum_t\lVert\mu_{t+1}-\mu_t\rVert^2$; and
- for temporally masked GPFA/AR posterior paths, block-time versus outside-block RMSE/coverage where emitted.

Full-path Mahalanobis and simultaneous-band diagnostics are useful extensions but are outside this bounded acceptance record; pointwise coverage and covariance-form oracles are retained here.

Coverage is repeated-simulation behavior conditional on fixed parameters. Selected-tau intervals omit timescale-selection uncertainty; learned baseline intervals omit loading-parameter uncertainty.

### Complete held-out trial NLL

Report marginal NLL per observed entry on untouched complete test trials for every explicit Gaussian observation model. Static independent-bin methods factor over times; GPFA and AR models use joint within-trial covariance. Poisson stress rows are labelled Gaussian approximation scores.

### Masked observation prediction

For each mask region, report joint predictive NLL per hidden entry, RMSE, and marginal 95% coverage for coherent probabilistic prediction methods. Mean predictive variance and hidden count are additionally required for the independent-channel baseline, where they make its fully hidden-channel prior behavior explicit. Separate complete-channel from contiguous-time-block results. Deterministic two-stage PCA/FA reconstructions report hidden-target RMSE only and do not fabricate raw-scale likelihood or coverage.

### Timescale recovery

For the selected grid pair, report selected values, absolute/relative errors, grid rank, train NLL, validation NLL, combined score, and gap to the next candidate. No coordinate-specific timescale recovery is reported in the equal-kernel learned condition.

### Residual diagnostics

For applicable Gaussian models, report residual mean and marginal variance, lag-1 autocorrelation, cross-channel residual covariance and maximum off-diagonal magnitude, plus explicit around/outside-changepoint summaries. Cholesky-whitened tails and residual Mahalanobis summaries are outside this bounded acceptance record and remain future extensions.

Residual whiteness under one ordering is a model diagnostic, not proof of the latent mechanism.

## Interpretation

The correct-model oracle tests finite Gaussian inference, not model discovery. The selected-tau baseline tests a tiny predeclared covariance-prior choice while holding the observation layer known. Learned static/two-stage baselines test loading-subspace estimation separately. RTS tests a first-order Markov covariance approximation, not the same squared-exponential prior.

A posterior mean can be smoother than the realized latent path because it averages under a smooth prior. That is expected Bayesian conditioning, not evidence that the method uncovered a transition law. A credible interval conditional on one selected tau pair is not total uncertainty. Better held-out prediction supports one bounded observation/prior combination and does not identify mechanism, dynamics, or unique latent coordinates.

::: {.callout-tip title="LDG synthesis"}
GPFA specifies covariance among latent values over time; LGSSM specifies a Markov transition and process noise. Both can yield retrospective Gaussian smoothers, but similar posterior plots do not make their model assumptions interchangeable.
:::

## What this toy model does NOT establish

- It does not discover a transition matrix, vector field, flow, or differential equation from the GP covariance.
- It does not make smooth posterior means measured latent trajectories.
- It does not make fixed-interval posterior estimates online or causal.
- It does not include parameter or model-selection uncertainty in plug-in intervals.
- It does not prove a unique loading basis or latent coordinate system.
- It does not identify coordinate-specific paths under equal kernels without an external convention.
- It does not validate diagonal residuals, Gaussian transformed counts, stationary kernels, or ignorable missingness outside tested conditions.
- It does not turn timescale recovery into a biological relaxation constant or mechanism.
- It does not infer intrinsic dimension, manifold structure, topology, or nonlinear geometry.
- It does not claim universal superiority of GPFA over FA, smoothing, or LGSSM.
- It does not call a finite grid unrestricted maximum likelihood.

## Extensions to real data

A real-data use must justify trial boundaries, simultaneity, binning, count transformation, offsets, loading dimension, residual covariance, kernel family, alignment, missingness, parameter sharing, and train/validation/test units. Low counts may require a count likelihood. Correlated residuals may require a richer observation model. Alignment uncertainty, nonstationarity, session drift, and condition changes need explicit models or sensitivity analyses.

Model selection should be nested; posterior calibration should use held-out channels/times/trials; and external behavior should be tested downstream without choosing latent representations on the final test set. Smooth trajectories should be presented with uncertainty and prior sensitivity, not as direct evidence of neural dynamics or mechanism.
