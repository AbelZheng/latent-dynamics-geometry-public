Graph Geometry Recovery Without Manifold Overclaim

Graph Geometry Recovery Without Manifold Overclaim

This L1/stable Candidate B Toy Model separates finite graph construction, shortest-path distance, classical multidimensional scaling (MDS), affinity- operator eigenspaces, diffusion-distance truncation, and held-out extension. It uses two simulators with known scoring targets: a unit-speed hairpin with analytic arc length, and a clean sampled circle whose uniform Gaussian affinity is circulant. Numerical values are deterministic outputs under master seed 20260820; the corrected generated artifact contains 197 finite, uniquely keyed metrics.

Assumed background: Euclidean distance, connected weighted graphs, all-pairs shortest paths, symmetric eigendecomposition, classical MDS, reversible Markov operators, principal angles, and leakage-safe train/test evaluation.

Question

The object asks two bounded questions.

  1. Hairpin: when observations trace a known folded interval, does a fixed \(k\)-nearest-neighbor Isomap graph approximate analytic arc-length distance and support a useful one-dimensional held-out extension beyond PCA and ambient- distance MDS? How do a connector gap, nonuniform sampling, reduced branch separation, bad \(k\), and anisotropic feature scaling break that result?
  2. Circle: for a finite clean uniform circle sample, does the declared \(\alpha=1\) Diffusion Maps operator recover the known first nonconstant two-dimensional Fourier eigenspace, how much diffusion distance is lost by retaining only two modes, and how accurately does normalized Nyström extension recover held-out midpoint angles? How do nonuniform density and extreme bandwidths change those finite-operator results?

The targets are declared simulator distances and finite graph/operator quantities. Graph success is not proof of topology, manifold structure, intrinsic dimension, geodesic or Riemannian geometry, unique coordinates, dynamics, mechanism, or causality.

Ground truth

Unit-speed vertical hairpin

Let

\[ r=0.175, \qquad L=6+\pi r=6.549778714378213. \]

The two straight branches have length \(3\) and separation \(2r=0.35\). For true arc-length parameter \(\ell\in[0,L]\), define

\[ c_r(\ell)= \begin{cases} (0,\ell), & 0\leq\ell\leq3,\\[3pt] \left(r+r\cos\theta,\ 3+r\sin\theta\right), &3<\ell\leq3+\pi r,\\[3pt] (2r,3-v), &3+\pi r<\ell\leq L, \end{cases} \]

where

\[ \theta=\pi-\frac{\ell-3}{r}, \qquad v=\ell-(3+\pi r). \]

The joins are continuous and \(\lVert c_r'(\ell)\rVert_2=1\) on the three smooth interiors. Therefore the simulator path distance is exactly

\[ d_\ell(i,j)=|\ell_i-\ell_j|. \]

A fixed QR-derived isometry embeds the plane in \(\mathbb R^3\). NumPy QR is applied to

\[ A= \begin{pmatrix} 1.0&0.3\\ -0.2&0.9\\ 0.45&-0.35 \end{pmatrix}, \]

and the retained two columns are

\[ Q= \begin{pmatrix} -0.8971226080&-0.3267089443\\ 0.1794245216&-0.8845629735\\ -0.4037051736& 0.3328807769 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad Q^\top Q=I_2. \]

With fixed offset \(o=(0.4,-0.7,0.2)^\top\),

\[ x^0(\ell)=Qc_r(\ell)+o, \qquad x(\ell)=x^0(\ell)+\epsilon, \qquad \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,0.01^2I_3). \]

The implementation retains \(\ell\) only for scoring and oracles. Graph fitting, PCA, MDS, and raw extension receive noisy observations only.

Circle and declared finite-operator target

The circle uses 96 clean, uniformly spaced training angles and 96 held-out midpoint angles:

\[ \theta_j=\frac{2\pi j}{96}, \qquad \theta_j^*=\frac{2\pi(j+1/2)}{96}, \qquad j=0,\ldots,95. \]

Planar points \((\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\) are embedded isometrically in \(\mathbb R^3\) using the same \(Q\), without the hairpin offset. For training points, the Gaussian affinity is

\[ W_{ij}=\exp\left(-\frac{\lVert y_i-y_j\rVert_2^2}{\varepsilon}\right). \]

Uniform circular spacing makes the clean distance and affinity matrices circulant. Hence

\[ \mathcal F_1 =\operatorname{span}\{(\cos\theta_j)_j,(\sin\theta_j)_j\} \]

is the declared first nonconstant finite-operator eigenspace. The implementation checks circulant structure, eigenvalue ordering, subspace angles, and an operator residual rather than hard-coding recovery. This is a finite matrix oracle, not a topology inference result.

Generative model

Hairpin splits

For nominal hairpin data, independently draw and sort

\[ \ell_i\sim\operatorname{Uniform}[0,L] \]

within each split. Split sizes are:

Split Rows Use
train 180 graph, PCA, MDS, and scoring-alignment fit
validation 80 fixed-rule extension evaluation
test 80 final fixed-rule extension evaluation

Separate Generator(PCG64(SeedSequence(words))) streams generate train, validation, test, control, and observation-noise draws. Sorting is only a storage convention; no method receives row order as a path edge.

Circle samples

The nominal training and midpoint grids are deterministic. The nonuniform circle control draws 96 deterministic seeded values, sorts them, and applies

\[ \theta=2\pi u^{2.4}, \qquad u\sim\operatorname{Uniform}[0,1]. \]

There is no circle validation grid and no circle metric-rescaling condition.

Generation, feature transformation, graph/affinity construction, spectral fitting, extension, scoring alignment, artifact writing, and verification are separate functions. Test truth never enters a graph, operator, embedding, or raw extension.

Parameters

Hairpin graph

  • dissimilarity: Euclidean distance in the supplied \(\mathbb R^3\) features;
  • nominal graph: undirected union \(k\)NN graph with \(k=6\);
  • neighbor candidates: set the distance diagonal to \(+\infty\) before stable mergesort, then retain the first \(k\) indices;
  • exact distance ties: stable sorting retains lower row indices first;
  • directed candidates are symmetrized by their union, with each retained edge assigned its exact ambient distance;
  • edge weight: ambient Euclidean distance;
  • shortest paths: deterministic vectorized Floyd–Warshall;
  • embedding dimension: one;
  • disconnected pairs: retain \(+\infty\) in diagnostics and do not pass them to classical MDS;
  • a long-arc-separation edge satisfies \[ |\ell_i-\ell_j|>0.70; \]
  • a material path shortcut satisfies \[ |\ell_i-\ell_j|-w_{ij}>0.50, \] where \(w_{ij}\) is the retained ambient edge weight; and
  • both rates use the number of unique undirected off-diagonal graph edges as their denominator.

Both edge labels use simulator truth only after graph construction. The long-arc label records separation without asserting underestimation; the term shortcut is reserved for the material path-underestimation criterion. No \(k\) is selected from \(\ell\), validation, or test results.

Classical MDS and extension

For finite distance matrix \(D\),

\[ J=I-\frac1n\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top, \qquad B=-\frac12JD^{\circ2}J. \]

For the leading eigenpair \((\lambda_1,v_1)\), the one-dimensional coordinate is

\[ z=\sqrt{\max(\lambda_1,0)}v_1. \]

The implementation reports two direct numerical diagnostics:

\[ \text{negative eigenmass}=\sum_{\lambda_j<0}|\lambda_j| \]

and

\[ \text{strain} =\sqrt{\operatorname{mean}\left(B-zz^\top\right)^2}. \]

These are not normalized fractions.

For a held-out point with squared distances \(\delta_*^2\) to training points, the implemented Gower extension forms

\[ b_* =-\frac12\left[ \delta_*^2 -\operatorname{mean}(\delta_*^2)\mathbf1 -\operatorname{rowmean}(D^{\circ2}) +\operatorname{mean}(D^{\circ2})\mathbf1 \right] \]

and returns

\[ z_*=\frac{b_*^\top v_1}{\sqrt{\max(\lambda_1,10^{-15})}}. \]

Ambient MDS uses \(\delta_{*j}=\lVert x_*-x_j\rVert_2\). Isomap attaches the new point to its six nearest training vertices without changing the training graph, then uses

\[ \delta_{*j} =\min_{i\in N_6(x_*)} \left(\lVert x_*-x_i\rVert_2+d_G(i,j)\right). \]

Hairpin scoring alignment

For each of PCA, ambient MDS, and Isomap, fit

\[ \ell_i=az_i+b \]

by least squares on training coordinates and training \(\ell\). Freeze \((a,b)\) and apply it unchanged to validation and test raw coordinates. The affine map handles sign, origin, and scale only for scoring; it is not part of the observation-only fit and does not identify a unique latent coordinate.

Diffusion Maps

The fixed nominal parameters are

\[ \varepsilon=0.18, \qquad \alpha=1, \qquad t=1, \qquad m=2. \]

Self-affinities are retained. Define

\[ q_i=\sum_jW_{ij}, \qquad K_{ij}=\frac{W_{ij}}{q_iq_j}, \qquad d_i=\sum_jK_{ij}, \]

\[ P=D^{-1}K, \qquad \pi_i=\frac{d_i}{\sum_rd_r}, \qquad S=D^{-1/2}KD^{-1/2}. \]

Diagonalize the real symmetric \(S\) with Euclidean-orthonormal eigenvectors \(\phi_l\). The implementation transforms them to right eigenfunctions

\[ \psi_l =\sqrt{\sum_i d_i}\,D^{-1/2}\phi_l, \]

so

\[ \psi_l^\top\Pi\psi_m=\delta_{lm}. \]

The two-mode coordinate excludes the trivial constant mode:

\[ \Phi_{1,2}(y_i) =\left(\lambda_1\psi_1(i),\lambda_2\psi_2(i)\right). \]

The full finite-operator diffusion distance uses all nonconstant modes,

\[ D_1(i,j)^2 =\sum_{l=1}^{95}\lambda_l^2 \left(\psi_l(i)-\psi_l(j)\right)^2, \]

and is independently recomputed from rows of \(P\) as

\[ D_1(i,j)^2 =\sum_k\frac{(P_{ik}-P_{jk})^2}{\pi_k}. \]

Diffusion time is one graph-operator step, not physical time or discovered dynamics (Coifman and Lafon 2006).

Normalized Nyström extension and circle alignment

For held-out \(y_*\), compute training affinities and

\[ q_*=\sum_jw_{*j}, \qquad k_{*j}=\frac{w_{*j}}{q_*q_j}, \qquad p_{*j}=\frac{k_{*j}}{\sum_rk_{*r}}. \]

For nonzero modes,

\[ \psi_l(y_*) =\frac1{\lambda_l}\sum_jp_{*j}\psi_l(j). \]

The two retained coordinates are linearly aligned to training rows \((\cos\theta_i,\sin\theta_i)\) by one unconstrained least-squares \(2\times2\) map. That same map scores all midpoint extensions. There is no second Procrustes, radial, angular, or validation alignment in the implementation. Spectral out-of-sample extension is an additional algorithm and need not equal a transductive full refit (Bengio et al. 2003).

Simulation

Nominal hairpin

Fit the training mean, rank-one PCA, ambient-distance MDS, and \(k=6\) Isomap on noisy training observations. Extend validation and test observations without rebuilding the graph.

Hairpin controls

The implemented controls are exact and predeclared:

  1. Sparse connector gap: rejection-sample 180 training \(\ell\) values outside \[ [3-0.18,\ 3+\pi r+0.18]. \]
  2. Reduced branch separation: set \[ r_r=0.06, \qquad L_r=6+\pi r_r=6.188495559215387, \] and preserve the nominal training quantiles by \[ \ell_r=\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{nominal}}}{L}\,L_r. \] Evaluate the same piecewise formula \(c_{r_r}\) on \([0,L_r]\) and use \(k=6\). This retains two straight branches of length 3 and endpoint \(c_{r_r}(L_r)=(0.12,0)\).
  3. Nonuniform sampling: draw \(u\sim U[0,1]\) and transform \[ \ell= \begin{cases} L\,0.55(u/0.65)^2,&u<0.65,\\ L\left[0.55+0.45((u-0.65)/0.35)^{0.55}\right],&u\geq0.65. \end{cases} \]
  4. Disconnected small neighborhood: nominal observations with union \(k=1\).
  5. Wrong larger neighborhood: nominal observations with union \(k=18\).
  6. Nearly complete graph: nominal observations with \(k=n_{\mathrm{train}}-2=178\).
  7. Anisotropic feature scaling: right-multiply nominal observed rows by \[ \operatorname{diag}(1,1,3) \] before distance and graph construction, then keep \(k=6\). This changes feature geometry and neighbor identities; it is not a harmless global scalar.

Circle conditions

  1. Uniform nominal: 96 clean training angles, \(\varepsilon=0.18\).
  2. Nonuniform density: seeded sorted \(2\pi u^{2.4}\) angles, \(\varepsilon=0.18\).
  3. Bandwidth too small: uniform angles with \(\varepsilon=0.005\).
  4. Bandwidth too large: uniform angles with \(\varepsilon=5.0\).

Failures are reported rather than tuned away. There is no circle metric-rescaling condition.

Expected behavior

  • The hairpin is unit-speed and the fixed \(Q\) is isometric, so noiseless path distance is exactly \(|\Delta\ell|\).
  • PCA and ambient-distance classical MDS should recover equivalent training one-dimensional sample coordinate subspaces for Euclidean distances. This is an implementation oracle, not a path-distance result.
  • A connected local graph without cross-branch edges can approximate analytic arc length better than ambient chords. Isomap separates graph construction, shortest paths, and MDS (Tenenbaum et al. 2000).
  • A sparse gap or \(k=1\) can disconnect the graph. Reduced separation, \(k=18\), or \(k=178\) can create material path shortcuts. Graph approximation depends on sampling, separation, curvature, and path conditions (Bernstein et al. 2000).
  • On the clean uniform circle, circulant affinity makes the cosine/sine span an exact finite-operator target up to floating point and basis rotation.
  • Two modes need not reproduce full finite-operator diffusion distance even when their eigenspace is recovered exactly.
  • Nonuniform density and extreme bandwidths change degrees, spectra, diffusion scale, truncation, and finite-sample eigenspaces. The \(\alpha=1\) continuum interpretation remains assumption-dependent rather than a finite guarantee (Coifman and Lafon 2006).

The empirical result, including contrary behavior, is retained without tuning.

Sanity checks

The executable verifier asserts only the following implemented oracles:

  1. hairpin join continuity, finite-difference unit speed, total length, and \(Q^\top Q=I\) within declared tolerances;
  2. graph adjacency symmetry, shortest-path symmetry and zero diagonal, finite distances for the nominal connected graph, explicit infinite distances for a disconnected control, and triangle inequality on 500 deterministic sampled triples;
  3. a synthetic exact-tie/duplicate \(k\)NN case with points \((0,0),(0,0),(1,0),(-1,0)\) verifies that no row selects itself, stable ties prefer lower indices, union symmetrization is exact, and retained edge weights equal the original distances exactly;
  4. the reduced-separation generator has \(L_r=6+\pi(0.06)\), two length-3 branches, endpoint \((0.12,0)\), zero domain violation, and reused nominal quantiles to numerical precision;
  5. PCA/ambient-MDS sample-projector equivalence and affine coordinate agreement;
  6. ambient-MDS and Isomap training-point Gower extension replay;
  7. uniform-circle affinity and Markov circulant discrepancies;
  8. Markov row sums, stationarity \(\pi^\top P=\pi^\top\), detailed balance, \(L^2(\pi)\) eigenfunction Gram matrix, and direct-row versus spectral diffusion distance;
  9. uniform-circle training-point Nyström replay;
  10. hairpin graph-distance and circle two-mode-projector invariance under deterministic relabeling;
  11. exact regeneration of hairpin splits/controls and circle grids;
  12. hairpin truth poisoning leaves fits and raw extensions unchanged;
  13. circle angle-truth poisoning changes truth while leaving observed train and midpoint points, fits, and raw Nyström extensions unchanged;
  14. test-observation poisoning leaves the hairpin training fit unchanged;
  15. anisotropic scaling changes at least one undirected neighbor edge;
  16. graph diagnostic/metric records, including both edge semantics, and the \(L^2(\pi)\) circle angle/residual records agree exactly between CSV and JSON;
  17. the exact CSV schema, finite values, nonempty labels, unique keys, artifact hashes, and byte-identical temporary regeneration.

These checks establish bounded numerical and leakage properties only.

Failure cases

  • Material shortcut hidden by a good plot: edges whose ambient weight underestimates simulator path separation by more than 0.50 can corrupt many graph distances while a one-dimensional display remains visually ordered.
  • Long separation confused with shortcut: \(|\Delta\ell|>0.70\) is separately reported but does not by itself prove material path underestimation.
  • Disconnected distance imputed: replacing \(+\infty\) by an arbitrary finite value invents a geometry; this implementation omits MDS for disconnected controls.
  • Negative eigenstructure hidden: the raw negative eigenmass and strain are reported rather than silently calling every distance matrix Euclidean.
  • Training truth used for neighbors: \(\ell\) and angle values are scoring and oracle inputs only.
  • Transductive refit called extension: held-out points are not inserted into the training graph or operator.
  • Unaligned axes compared literally: train-fitted affine/linear maps handle coordinate conventions only at scoring time.
  • Two-mode coordinates called full diffusion geometry: truncation error is explicitly measured against all finite nonconstant modes.
  • Density normalization treated as a finite guarantee: \(\alpha=1\) does not prove density-free geometry in the nonuniform finite sample.
  • Circle eigenspace called topology recovery: the result follows from a generated circulant matrix, not a topology estimator.
  • Diffusion time called physical time: \(t=1\) is one Markov-operator step.
  • Feature rescaling ignored: anisotropic scaling changed 85 undirected neighbor edges in this realization.

Recovery task

Hairpin baselines and Isomap

The mean baseline predicts the training mean of \(\ell\) only for scoring. PCA centers training observations and projects onto the leading right singular vector. Ambient MDS applies classical MDS directly to training Euclidean distances. Isomap constructs the union-\(k\) graph, computes all-pairs shortest paths, and applies the same MDS routine only when connected. Held-out points use the fixed Gower rules above.

Circle Diffusion Maps

The circle analysis constructs the dense Gaussian affinity with self-loops, applies \(\alpha=1\) normalization, diagonalizes the symmetric conjugate, normalizes right eigenfunctions in \(L^2(\pi)\), and retains the first two nonconstant modes. It computes full and truncated diffusion distances and uses the fixed normalized Nyström extension without refitting.

Candidate analysis methods

Hairpin

  1. training-mean scoring reference;
  2. rank-one PCA;
  3. ambient-distance classical MDS in one dimension;
  4. union-\(k\) Isomap plus one-dimensional classical MDS; and
  5. direct graph diagnostics without embedding.

Circle

  1. raw Gaussian affinity and normalized Markov diagnostics;
  2. full \(\alpha=1\) finite-operator diffusion distance;
  3. first-two-nonconstant-mode Diffusion Maps at \(t=1\);
  4. normalized Nyström midpoint extension; and
  5. direct Fourier-subspace/operator diagnostics.

No method uses truth to choose \(k\), \(\varepsilon\), retained modes, graph edges, or extension parameters.

Implemented hairpin metrics

For finite unordered training pairs, graph-distance relative RMSE is

\[ \frac{\sqrt{\operatorname{mean}(d_G-d_\ell)^2}} {\sqrt{\operatorname{mean}d_\ell^2}}, \]

and Pearson correlation is reported with it. Each graph condition also reports edge count; long-arc-separation edge count/rate/threshold/denominator; material path shortcut count/rate/underestimation threshold/denominator; component count; and finite/infinite pair counts. Connected Isomap conditions additionally report raw MDS negative eigenmass and strain. The anisotropic condition reports undirected neighbor-edge symmetric-difference count and rate. The reduced- separation and exact-tie generator/oracle quantities are also emitted as metrics.

Nominal mean, PCA, ambient MDS, and Isomap report only train/validation/test affine-aligned coordinate RMSE. No endpoint/interior, MAE, coordinate correlation, degree-quantile, or extra stress metric is emitted.

Implemented circle metrics

The uniform condition reports two \(L^2(\pi)\) principal angles, the weighted \(P\)-invariant-subspace residual, two-mode truncation relative RMSE, direct-versus-spectral maximum discrepancy, midpoint Nyström RMSE, the leading eight eigenvalues, and five Markov/operator oracle discrepancies. Each circle control reports the same weighted residual, truncation relative RMSE, full-spectrum pairwise distance RMS, four leading eigenvalues, and two weighted principal angles. No radial/angular extension metric or circle validation metric is emitted.

The declared \(L^2(\pi)\) principal angles are computed between \(\sqrt\Pi\Psi_{1:2}\) and \(\sqrt\Pi C\), where \(C=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\). The implemented invariant-subspace residual uses \(P\) and is

\[ \min_A\frac{\lVert PC-CA\rVert_{F,\pi}}{\lVert C\rVert_{F,\pi}}, \qquad \lVert M\rVert_{F,\pi}=\lVert\sqrt\Pi M\rVert_F. \]

Interpretation

The nominal hairpin graph closely approximated this simulator’s arc-length distance and its frozen Isomap extension retained low RMSE on independent validation and test samples. PCA and ambient MDS were equivalent linear/chord baselines and had much larger aligned coordinate error. The negative controls show that this result depended on connector coverage, branch separation, neighborhood size, sampling density, and feature metric.

The uniform circle supplied an algebraic finite-operator oracle: its first nonconstant right-eigenfunction eigenspace matched the cosine/sine span in the \(L^2(\pi)\) representation, and midpoint Nyström extension was accurate to floating-point precision. Nevertheless, two modes omitted substantial full diffusion-distance content at \(\varepsilon=0.18\). Nonuniform density rotated the finite leading eigenspace away from the uniform Fourier target, while extreme bandwidths changed the spectrum and diffusion scale even when circulant symmetry preserved the Fourier axes.

Graph shortest paths remain finite combinatorial distances, not automatically smooth geodesics. Train-fitted alignments change only scoring coordinates and do not identify scientifically privileged axes.

WarningWhat this does NOT imply

Accurate graph distances, low MDS strain, Fourier-eigenspace recovery, a circular plot, or low held-out extension error does not prove topology, manifold structure, intrinsic dimension, geodesic or Riemannian recovery, unique coordinates, dynamics, mechanism, causality, or biological validity.

What this toy model does NOT establish

  • It does not prove that unknown data are sampled from a manifold.
  • It does not recover interval or circle topology from real data.
  • It does not estimate model-free intrinsic dimension.
  • It does not make graph shortest paths smooth or globally minimizing geodesics.
  • It does not estimate a Riemannian metric tensor, curvature, tangent spaces, or an atlas.
  • It does not make graph connectivity evidence for connected latent topology.
  • It does not make a repeated eigenspace two unique coordinates.
  • It does not make diffusion time physical time.
  • It does not discover dynamics, a vector field, transition law, mechanism, or causal structure.
  • It does not establish universal superiority of Isomap or Diffusion Maps.
  • It does not guarantee equality between frozen extension and transductive refit.

Extensions to real data

Real-data graph geometry requires an independently justified observational unit, feature representation, scaling, missing-data policy, distance, neighborhood or bandwidth, density normalization, component policy, coordinate count, and out-of-sample use. Dependence units such as subjects, sessions, trials, or trajectories should be split intact. Building one graph on train and test is transductive and cannot be reported as held-out deployment.

Before interpreting coordinates, report neighbor examples, components, edge and degree distributions, negative eigenstructure, spectral stability, truncation, and metric/hyperparameter sensitivity. Without simulator truth, graph quality requires external scientific justification, stability, or held-out tasks rather than a compelling visualization.

# Graph Geometry Recovery Without Manifold Overclaim

This L1/stable Candidate B Toy Model separates finite graph construction,
shortest-path distance, classical multidimensional scaling (MDS), affinity-
operator eigenspaces, diffusion-distance truncation, and held-out extension. It
uses two simulators with known scoring targets: a unit-speed hairpin with analytic
arc length, and a clean sampled circle whose uniform Gaussian affinity is
circulant. Numerical values are deterministic outputs under master seed
`20260820`; the corrected generated artifact contains 197 finite, uniquely keyed
metrics.

**Assumed background:** Euclidean distance, connected weighted graphs,
all-pairs shortest paths, symmetric eigendecomposition, classical MDS, reversible
Markov operators, principal angles, and leakage-safe train/test evaluation.

## Question

The object asks two bounded questions.

1. **Hairpin:** when observations trace a known folded interval, does a fixed
   $k$-nearest-neighbor Isomap graph approximate analytic arc-length distance and
   support a useful one-dimensional held-out extension beyond PCA and ambient-
   distance MDS? How do a connector gap, nonuniform sampling, reduced branch
   separation, bad $k$, and anisotropic feature scaling break that result?
2. **Circle:** for a finite clean uniform circle sample, does the declared
   $\alpha=1$ Diffusion Maps operator recover the known first nonconstant
   two-dimensional Fourier eigenspace, how much diffusion distance is lost by
   retaining only two modes, and how accurately does normalized Nyström
   extension recover held-out midpoint angles? How do nonuniform density and
   extreme bandwidths change those finite-operator results?

The targets are declared simulator distances and finite graph/operator
quantities. Graph success is not proof of topology, manifold structure,
intrinsic dimension, geodesic or Riemannian geometry, unique coordinates,
dynamics, mechanism, or causality.

## Ground truth

### Unit-speed vertical hairpin

Let

$$
r=0.175,
\qquad
L=6+\pi r=6.549778714378213.
$$

The two straight branches have length $3$ and separation $2r=0.35$. For true
arc-length parameter $\ell\in[0,L]$, define

$$
c_r(\ell)=
\begin{cases}
(0,\ell), & 0\leq\ell\leq3,\\[3pt]
\left(r+r\cos\theta,\ 3+r\sin\theta\right),
&3<\ell\leq3+\pi r,\\[3pt]
(2r,3-v), &3+\pi r<\ell\leq L,
\end{cases}
$$

where

$$
\theta=\pi-\frac{\ell-3}{r},
\qquad
v=\ell-(3+\pi r).
$$

The joins are continuous and $\lVert c_r'(\ell)\rVert_2=1$ on the three smooth
interiors. Therefore the simulator path distance is exactly

$$
d_\ell(i,j)=|\ell_i-\ell_j|.
$$

A fixed QR-derived isometry embeds the plane in $\mathbb R^3$. NumPy QR is
applied to

$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1.0&0.3\\
-0.2&0.9\\
0.45&-0.35
\end{pmatrix},
$$

and the retained two columns are

$$
Q=
\begin{pmatrix}
-0.8971226080&-0.3267089443\\
 0.1794245216&-0.8845629735\\
-0.4037051736& 0.3328807769
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad Q^\top Q=I_2.
$$

With fixed offset $o=(0.4,-0.7,0.2)^\top$,

$$
x^0(\ell)=Qc_r(\ell)+o,
\qquad
x(\ell)=x^0(\ell)+\epsilon,
\qquad
\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,0.01^2I_3).
$$

The implementation retains $\ell$ only for scoring and oracles. Graph fitting,
PCA, MDS, and raw extension receive noisy observations only.

### Circle and declared finite-operator target

The circle uses 96 clean, uniformly spaced training angles and 96 held-out
midpoint angles:

$$
\theta_j=\frac{2\pi j}{96},
\qquad
\theta_j^*=\frac{2\pi(j+1/2)}{96},
\qquad j=0,\ldots,95.
$$

Planar points $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ are embedded isometrically in
$\mathbb R^3$ using the same $Q$, without the hairpin offset. For training
points, the Gaussian affinity is

$$
W_{ij}=\exp\left(-\frac{\lVert y_i-y_j\rVert_2^2}{\varepsilon}\right).
$$

Uniform circular spacing makes the clean distance and affinity matrices
circulant. Hence

$$
\mathcal F_1
=\operatorname{span}\{(\cos\theta_j)_j,(\sin\theta_j)_j\}
$$

is the declared first nonconstant finite-operator eigenspace. The implementation
checks circulant structure, eigenvalue ordering, subspace angles, and an operator
residual rather than hard-coding recovery. This is a finite matrix oracle, not a
topology inference result.

## Parameters

### Hairpin graph

- dissimilarity: Euclidean distance in the supplied $\mathbb R^3$ features;
- nominal graph: undirected **union** $k$NN graph with $k=6$;
- neighbor candidates: set the distance diagonal to $+\infty$ before stable
  mergesort, then retain the first $k$ indices;
- exact distance ties: stable sorting retains lower row indices first;
- directed candidates are symmetrized by their union, with each retained edge
  assigned its exact ambient distance;
- edge weight: ambient Euclidean distance;
- shortest paths: deterministic vectorized Floyd--Warshall;
- embedding dimension: one;
- disconnected pairs: retain $+\infty$ in diagnostics and do not pass them to
  classical MDS;
- a **long-arc-separation edge** satisfies
  $$
  |\ell_i-\ell_j|>0.70;
  $$
- a **material path shortcut** satisfies
  $$
  |\ell_i-\ell_j|-w_{ij}>0.50,
  $$
  where $w_{ij}$ is the retained ambient edge weight; and
- both rates use the number of unique undirected off-diagonal graph edges as
  their denominator.

Both edge labels use simulator truth only after graph construction. The
long-arc label records separation without asserting underestimation; the term
**shortcut** is reserved for the material path-underestimation criterion. No
$k$ is selected from $\ell$, validation, or test results.

### Classical MDS and extension

For finite distance matrix $D$,

$$
J=I-\frac1n\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top,
\qquad
B=-\frac12JD^{\circ2}J.
$$

For the leading eigenpair $(\lambda_1,v_1)$, the one-dimensional coordinate is

$$
z=\sqrt{\max(\lambda_1,0)}v_1.
$$

The implementation reports two direct numerical diagnostics:

$$
\text{negative eigenmass}=\sum_{\lambda_j<0}|\lambda_j|
$$

and

$$
\text{strain}
=\sqrt{\operatorname{mean}\left(B-zz^\top\right)^2}.
$$

These are not normalized fractions.

For a held-out point with squared distances $\delta_*^2$ to training points, the
implemented Gower extension forms

$$
b_*
=-\frac12\left[
\delta_*^2
-\operatorname{mean}(\delta_*^2)\mathbf1
-\operatorname{rowmean}(D^{\circ2})
+\operatorname{mean}(D^{\circ2})\mathbf1
\right]
$$

and returns

$$
z_*=\frac{b_*^\top v_1}{\sqrt{\max(\lambda_1,10^{-15})}}.
$$

Ambient MDS uses $\delta_{*j}=\lVert x_*-x_j\rVert_2$. Isomap attaches the new
point to its six nearest training vertices without changing the training graph,
then uses

$$
\delta_{*j}
=\min_{i\in N_6(x_*)}
\left(\lVert x_*-x_i\rVert_2+d_G(i,j)\right).
$$

### Hairpin scoring alignment

For each of PCA, ambient MDS, and Isomap, fit

$$
\ell_i=az_i+b
$$

by least squares on training coordinates and training $\ell$. Freeze $(a,b)$ and
apply it unchanged to validation and test raw coordinates. The affine map handles
sign, origin, and scale only for scoring; it is not part of the observation-only
fit and does not identify a unique latent coordinate.

### Diffusion Maps

The fixed nominal parameters are

$$
\varepsilon=0.18,
\qquad
\alpha=1,
\qquad
t=1,
\qquad
m=2.
$$

Self-affinities are retained. Define

$$
q_i=\sum_jW_{ij},
\qquad
K_{ij}=\frac{W_{ij}}{q_iq_j},
\qquad
d_i=\sum_jK_{ij},
$$

$$
P=D^{-1}K,
\qquad
\pi_i=\frac{d_i}{\sum_rd_r},
\qquad
S=D^{-1/2}KD^{-1/2}.
$$

Diagonalize the real symmetric $S$ with Euclidean-orthonormal eigenvectors
$\phi_l$. The implementation transforms them to right eigenfunctions

$$
\psi_l
=\sqrt{\sum_i d_i}\,D^{-1/2}\phi_l,
$$

so

$$
\psi_l^\top\Pi\psi_m=\delta_{lm}.
$$

The two-mode coordinate excludes the trivial constant mode:

$$
\Phi_{1,2}(y_i)
=\left(\lambda_1\psi_1(i),\lambda_2\psi_2(i)\right).
$$

The full finite-operator diffusion distance uses all nonconstant modes,

$$
D_1(i,j)^2
=\sum_{l=1}^{95}\lambda_l^2
\left(\psi_l(i)-\psi_l(j)\right)^2,
$$

and is independently recomputed from rows of $P$ as

$$
D_1(i,j)^2
=\sum_k\frac{(P_{ik}-P_{jk})^2}{\pi_k}.
$$

Diffusion time is one graph-operator step, not physical time or discovered
dynamics [@CoifmanLafon2006].

### Normalized Nyström extension and circle alignment

For held-out $y_*$, compute training affinities and

$$
q_*=\sum_jw_{*j},
\qquad
k_{*j}=\frac{w_{*j}}{q_*q_j},
\qquad
p_{*j}=\frac{k_{*j}}{\sum_rk_{*r}}.
$$

For nonzero modes,

$$
\psi_l(y_*)
=\frac1{\lambda_l}\sum_jp_{*j}\psi_l(j).
$$

The two retained coordinates are linearly aligned to training rows
$(\cos\theta_i,\sin\theta_i)$ by one unconstrained least-squares $2\times2$ map.
That same map scores all midpoint extensions. There is no second Procrustes,
radial, angular, or validation alignment in the implementation. Spectral
out-of-sample extension is an additional algorithm and need not equal a
transductive full refit [@BengioEtAl2003].

## Expected behavior

- The hairpin is unit-speed and the fixed $Q$ is isometric, so noiseless path
  distance is exactly $|\Delta\ell|$.
- PCA and ambient-distance classical MDS should recover equivalent training
  one-dimensional sample coordinate subspaces for Euclidean distances. This is
  an implementation oracle, not a path-distance result.
- A connected local graph without cross-branch edges can approximate analytic
  arc length better than ambient chords. Isomap separates graph construction,
  shortest paths, and MDS [@TenenbaumDeSilvaLangford2000].
- A sparse gap or $k=1$ can disconnect the graph. Reduced separation, $k=18$,
  or $k=178$ can create material path shortcuts. Graph approximation depends on sampling,
  separation, curvature, and path conditions [@BernsteinEtAl2000].
- On the clean uniform circle, circulant affinity makes the cosine/sine span an
  exact finite-operator target up to floating point and basis rotation.
- Two modes need not reproduce full finite-operator diffusion distance even when
  their eigenspace is recovered exactly.
- Nonuniform density and extreme bandwidths change degrees, spectra, diffusion
  scale, truncation, and finite-sample eigenspaces. The $\alpha=1$ continuum
  interpretation remains assumption-dependent rather than a finite guarantee
  [@CoifmanLafon2006].

The empirical result, including contrary behavior, is retained without tuning.

## Failure cases

- **Material shortcut hidden by a good plot:** edges whose ambient weight
  underestimates simulator path separation by more than 0.50 can corrupt many
  graph distances while a one-dimensional display remains visually ordered.
- **Long separation confused with shortcut:** $|\Delta\ell|>0.70$ is separately
  reported but does not by itself prove material path underestimation.
- **Disconnected distance imputed:** replacing $+\infty$ by an arbitrary finite
  value invents a geometry; this implementation omits MDS for disconnected
  controls.
- **Negative eigenstructure hidden:** the raw negative eigenmass and strain are
  reported rather than silently calling every distance matrix Euclidean.
- **Training truth used for neighbors:** $\ell$ and angle values are scoring and
  oracle inputs only.
- **Transductive refit called extension:** held-out points are not inserted into
  the training graph or operator.
- **Unaligned axes compared literally:** train-fitted affine/linear maps handle
  coordinate conventions only at scoring time.
- **Two-mode coordinates called full diffusion geometry:** truncation error is
  explicitly measured against all finite nonconstant modes.
- **Density normalization treated as a finite guarantee:** $\alpha=1$ does not
  prove density-free geometry in the nonuniform finite sample.
- **Circle eigenspace called topology recovery:** the result follows from a
  generated circulant matrix, not a topology estimator.
- **Diffusion time called physical time:** $t=1$ is one Markov-operator step.
- **Feature rescaling ignored:** anisotropic scaling changed 85 undirected
  neighbor edges in this realization.

## Recovery task

### Hairpin baselines and Isomap

The mean baseline predicts the training mean of $\ell$ only for scoring. PCA
centers training observations and projects onto the leading right singular
vector. Ambient MDS applies classical MDS directly to training Euclidean
distances. Isomap constructs the union-$k$ graph, computes all-pairs shortest
paths, and applies the same MDS routine only when connected. Held-out points use
the fixed Gower rules above.

### Circle Diffusion Maps

The circle analysis constructs the dense Gaussian affinity with self-loops,
applies $\alpha=1$ normalization, diagonalizes the symmetric conjugate,
normalizes right eigenfunctions in $L^2(\pi)$, and retains the first two
nonconstant modes. It computes full and truncated diffusion distances and uses
the fixed normalized Nyström extension without refitting.

### Implemented circle metrics

The uniform condition reports two $L^2(\pi)$ principal angles, the weighted
$P$-invariant-subspace residual,
two-mode truncation relative RMSE, direct-versus-spectral maximum discrepancy,
midpoint Nyström RMSE, the leading eight eigenvalues, and five Markov/operator
oracle discrepancies. Each circle control reports the same weighted residual,
truncation relative RMSE, full-spectrum pairwise distance RMS, four leading
eigenvalues, and two weighted principal angles. No radial/angular extension
metric or circle validation metric is emitted.

The declared $L^2(\pi)$ principal angles are computed between
$\sqrt\Pi\Psi_{1:2}$ and $\sqrt\Pi C$, where
$C=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$. The implemented invariant-subspace residual uses
$P$ and is

$$
\min_A\frac{\lVert PC-CA\rVert_{F,\pi}}{\lVert C\rVert_{F,\pi}},
\qquad
\lVert M\rVert_{F,\pi}=\lVert\sqrt\Pi M\rVert_F.
$$

## Interpretation

The nominal hairpin graph closely approximated this simulator's arc-length
distance and its frozen Isomap extension retained low RMSE on independent
validation and test samples. PCA and ambient MDS were equivalent linear/chord
baselines and had much larger aligned coordinate error. The negative controls
show that this result depended on connector coverage, branch separation,
neighborhood size, sampling density, and feature metric.

The uniform circle supplied an algebraic finite-operator oracle: its first
nonconstant right-eigenfunction eigenspace matched the cosine/sine span in the
$L^2(\pi)$ representation, and midpoint Nyström extension was accurate to
floating-point precision. Nevertheless, two modes
omitted substantial full diffusion-distance content at $\varepsilon=0.18$.
Nonuniform density rotated the finite leading eigenspace away from the uniform
Fourier target, while extreme bandwidths changed the spectrum and diffusion
scale even when circulant symmetry preserved the Fourier axes.

Graph shortest paths remain finite combinatorial distances, not automatically
smooth geodesics. Train-fitted alignments change only scoring coordinates and do
not identify scientifically privileged axes.

::: {.callout-warning title="What this does NOT imply"}
Accurate graph distances, low MDS strain, Fourier-eigenspace recovery, a circular
plot, or low held-out extension error does not prove topology, manifold
structure, intrinsic dimension, geodesic or Riemannian recovery, unique
coordinates, dynamics, mechanism, causality, or biological validity.
:::

## What this toy model does NOT establish

- It does not prove that unknown data are sampled from a manifold.
- It does not recover interval or circle topology from real data.
- It does not estimate model-free intrinsic dimension.
- It does not make graph shortest paths smooth or globally minimizing geodesics.
- It does not estimate a Riemannian metric tensor, curvature, tangent spaces, or
  an atlas.
- It does not make graph connectivity evidence for connected latent topology.
- It does not make a repeated eigenspace two unique coordinates.
- It does not make diffusion time physical time.
- It does not discover dynamics, a vector field, transition law, mechanism, or
  causal structure.
- It does not establish universal superiority of Isomap or Diffusion Maps.
- It does not guarantee equality between frozen extension and transductive refit.

## Extensions to real data

Real-data graph geometry requires an independently justified observational unit,
feature representation, scaling, missing-data policy, distance, neighborhood or
bandwidth, density normalization, component policy, coordinate count, and
out-of-sample use. Dependence units such as subjects, sessions, trials, or
trajectories should be split intact. Building one graph on train and test is
transductive and cannot be reported as held-out deployment.

Before interpreting coordinates, report neighbor examples, components, edge and
degree distributions, negative eigenstructure, spectral stability, truncation,
