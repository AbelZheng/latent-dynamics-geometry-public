P4-U02 — Static Linear Latent Recovery Benchmark

P4-U02 — Static Linear Latent Recovery Benchmark

Scientific question

Across a bounded grid of independent rank-two Gaussian-factor simulations and declared misspecification controls, when do mean and rank-matched projection references, ordinary PCA, probabilistic PCA (PPCA), bounded diagonal-noise factor analysis (FA), and known-parameter Gaussian references recover the different targets their criteria define, and where are they inapplicable, numerically nonconvergent, or observably brittle?

This is not a shared-target leaderboard. In particular, ordinary PCA is judged against the population principal subspace of the observable covariance, while FA loading recovery is judged against the common-loading structure only when the diagonal-residual model and the declared structural-identification diagnostic apply. Reconstruction, likelihood, covariance recovery, subspace recovery, uniqueness recovery, convergence, and leakage are separate tasks and are never collapsed into one score.

Ground truth

Each independent row is generated in ambient dimension \(p=8\) with latent rank \(q=2\):

\[ z_i\sim\mathcal N(0,I_2),\qquad \epsilon_i\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_\epsilon),\qquad x_i=\Lambda z_i+\epsilon_i. \]

The retained truth includes \(z\), \(\Lambda\), the common covariance \(C_{\mathrm{common}}=\Lambda\Lambda^\top\), residual covariance \(\Sigma_\epsilon\), observable covariance \(C=C_{\mathrm{common}}+\Sigma_\epsilon\), the loading subspace \(\operatorname{col}(\Lambda)\), and the rank-two population principal subspace of \(C\). These are not interchangeable targets.

Population parameters and latent arrays are available only to generation, scoring, and implementation-oracle checks. They are not supplied to lawful preprocessing, fitting, validation selection, or held-out recovery. The executable reuses the stable numerical definitions and routines in toy-models/static-linear-latent-recovery.py without editing the stable Toy Model or changing its scientific conclusions.

Identification, recovery, and convergence are different claims

Three boundaries are recorded separately:

  1. Structural identification applicability. For diagonal-noise FA, uniqueness and common-loading targets are eligible only when the residual covariance is diagonal and the displayed loading matrix passes the retained Anderson–Rubin-style sufficient row-deletion rank diagnostic. Loading columns remain equivalent under orthogonal rotation.
  2. Finite-sample recovery. Principal angles and covariance errors describe one bounded fitted sample relative to known synthetic truth. Identification does not guarantee that these errors are small.
  3. Optimizer convergence. The bounded EM implementation can reach its iteration limit or a numerical boundary. Convergence is a computational label, not proof of structural identification, population correctness, or global maximum likelihood; nonconvergence is not evidence that the population model class cannot represent the truth.

Generation

Generation receives only a scenario identifier, replicate identifier, split, and deterministic seed words. It produces independent train, validation, and test observations and retains truth in a separate scoring payload. Outlier contamination and residual correlation are applied only in their predeclared scenario classes. Generation does not call preprocessing, fitting, selection, recovery, or scoring.

The primary model uses the stable Toy Model’s deterministic rank-two loading basis and heterogeneous diagonal uniquenesses. Scenario-specific changes are fixed before outcome interpretation and are recorded in manifest.json.

The grid is a bounded core-plus-stress design rather than a complete factorial experiment. It covers every Phase 4 contract factor while keeping independent replicates and byte-level regeneration practical.

Changing one named scenario can also change the observable covariance, eigengap, or effective signal-to-noise ratio. Consequently, differences across scenario rows are conditional robustness observations, not isolated causal effects of one factor. Exact scenario values, split sizes, expected applicability, replicate count, and seed mapping are frozen in the manifest.

Negative controls and stress cases

  • Fixed and seed-derived random rank-two projections show what rank matching alone can achieve without estimating a population target. There is exactly one random basis for each scenario-replicate.
  • Correlated residuals deliberately violate diagonal-noise FA. A fitted factor under this condition cannot be called a recovered common cause.
  • High-variance nuisance tests whether a variance-defined PCA target diverges from the unchanged loading target; correct PCA recovery is not a semantic error.
  • Near-zero uniqueness stresses positivity floors, boundary behavior, and finite iteration budgets.
  • Outliers violate the clean Gaussian law. The true clean parameters remain a nominal reference only, not the true distribution of contaminated data.
  • Pooled train/test preprocessing is explicitly invalid. Leakage is proved by dependence on poisoned test observations, not by whether one finite test score improves.

Failures are retained and labelled rather than tuned away.

Replicates and deterministic seeds

Each of the 11 declared scenarios uses four independent deterministic replicates. Seed words include the master seed, a stable scenario code, replicate, split, and any sequence or baseline index needed by the generator. Mapping does not depend on execution order. Methods share a replicate’s generated data to support paired, target-specific comparisons, while different replicates use independent random streams.

One raw record is produced per scenario \(\times\) replicate \(\times\) method \(\times\) metric. Its value is computed from the metric-specific provenance declared under Scoring—test rows, validation rows, training optimization, population truth, or the train/test poison intervention as appropriate. Replicate identity is never discarded.

Split discipline

Train, validation, and test samples are generated independently. Training data fit means, lawful preprocessing, PCA/PPCA, and each bounded FA candidate. Validation observations select only among the predeclared FA floors and restarts. Test observations are used only by the frozen test-time recovery or likelihood calculation. Retained clean test signal and population parameters are joined only in the separate metric-specific scoring stage.

Lawful selection accepts restricted train/validation observation payloads. It has no capability to read test observations or any latent/population truth. Poison checks replace excluded test observations and retained latent arrays and require lawful fitted states, selected FA settings, and observation-only recoveries to remain unchanged. The pooled-scaling negative control is kept separate and explicitly labelled invalid.

Methods and information sets

  • Training mean: rank-zero reconstruction reference fitted on training observations.
  • Fixed rank-two projection: predeclared orthonormal compression reference; it is not fitted to outcomes.
  • Random rank-two projection: exactly one rank-two orthonormal basis is deterministically derived from the scenario and replicate seed, independently of that replicate’s generated observations. It is a rank-matched reference, not a validation-selected method. Across replicates, both the generated data and the seed-derived basis vary jointly; the replicate summary therefore does not isolate basis variability from sampling variability or average a family of bases within one replicate.
  • Ordinary covariance PCA: deterministic rank-two orthogonal projection fitted on training observations. Its population target is the leading rank-two eigenspace of the observable covariance under the declared preprocessing and Euclidean metric.
  • PPCA: the isotropic-residual Gaussian restriction \(C=WW^\top+\sigma^2I\). Its maximum-likelihood loading space is the PCA principal subspace, but it additionally supplies a Gaussian covariance, likelihood, and posterior mean. It is not general diagonal-noise FA, and its likelihood does not retroactively make ordinary PCA probabilistic.
  • Bounded diagonal-noise FA: a rank-two Gaussian model \(C=\Lambda\Lambda^\top+\operatorname{diag}(\psi)\) fitted by deterministic bounded EM candidates and validation selection. Loading columns are compared only through rotation-invariant subspace/common-covariance targets.
  • True-parameter Gaussian reference: uses known simulator parameters for likelihood and posterior reconstruction. It is an unavailable implementation/reference oracle, not a deployable fitted competitor. Under outlier contamination it is named and interpreted as a nominal clean Gaussian reference.

All lawful methods use the same frozen test observations. Population eigenspaces, loadings, covariances, uniquenesses, and latent scores are scoring truth or oracle inputs, never fitting information.

Parameter selection

PCA and closed-form PPCA use the predeclared rank two and require no validation selection. Fixed/random projections are frozen before data generation. For FA, the training split fits every predeclared uniqueness-floor \(\times\) restart candidate; validation Gaussian NLL selects one candidate with a deterministic tie rule. The selected floor and restart are frozen before test recovery and scoring.

This finite candidate set is a bounded numerical procedure. It does not prove global optimization, and validation selection cannot turn a misspecified diagonal residual model into a correct one.

Inference

For reconstruction methods, recovery receives frozen preprocessing and fitted states plus test observations. PCA and projection baselines return Euclidean reconstructions in original coordinates. PPCA and FA return model-conditional Gaussian posterior-mean reconstructions. These are different estimators even at the same rank.

No method receives test latent truth. The true-parameter Gaussian reference is invoked through an explicitly oracle-labelled path. There is no temporal information set: observations are independent static rows, and their ordering has no scientific meaning.

Scoring

Raw rows use metric-specific target metadata. The following task families remain separate:

Raw metric Provenance, target, and interpretation
heldout_reconstruction_mse Test observations and frozen reconstructions; original-coordinate observed reconstruction, including contaminated observations in outliers; not loading or covariance recovery
latent_signal_reconstruction_mse Test clean common signal \(\Lambda z\) retained by the generator, compared with the fitted reconstruction after subtracting the declared population mean; defined for every method that returns a finite test reconstruction, including mean, fixed/random projection, PCA, PPCA, selected FA, and the true-parameter reference. The two scaled-PCA controls are applicable only in preprocessing_leakage. In outliers, observations and fitted reconstructions include contamination while the target remains the uncontaminated \(\Lambda z\), so the metric measures recovery of the clean simulated common contribution rather than reconstruction of the contaminated observation law
heldout_gaussian_nll Test observations under a frozen PPCA, FA, or true-parameter Gaussian density; ordinary PCA and pure projections do not define this score. In outliers, this is a Gaussian score on contaminated observations, and the clean true-parameter model is only a nominal reference
covariance_relative_frobenius Fitted model covariance versus population observable covariance; for outliers, the target is explicitly the nominal clean Gaussian covariance rather than the covariance of the contaminated law
principal_subspace_angle_max_degrees Training-fitted PCA/PPCA space versus the population rank-two principal subspace of observable \(C\); the value combines fitted training output with population scoring truth, not test observations
loading_subspace_angle_max_degrees Training/validation-selected FA loading space versus \(\operatorname{col}(\Lambda)\), or the exact reference against itself, only where the diagonal-residual and sufficient-rank identification conditions apply; population-truth scoring, not a test-split loss
uniqueness_rmse Selected FA diagonal uniqueness versus population diagonal uniqueness only where the same identification conditions apply; the implementation emits RMSE, not uniqueness relative error
selected_validation_nll Validation observations and the selected bounded FA candidate; a selection diagnostic, not held-out test performance
optimizer_iterations Training optimization history of the selected FA candidate; a computational diagnostic. A run that reaches the bound is labelled nonconvergence
identification_applicable Population scenario specification plus the sufficient rank diagnostic; a binary applicability record, not empirical recovery performance
preprocessing_test_poison_parameter_change Difference between preprocessing/model parameters before and after deterministic test poisoning; uses training and test observations only for the declared leakage control and is not a candidate-method score

Provenance is therefore metric-specific: only the held-out reconstruction, clean-signal reconstruction, and Gaussian NLL metrics are direct test-row aggregates. Subspace, covariance, uniqueness, and identification metrics join fitted states to population truth; validation NLL comes from validation data; optimizer iterations come from training optimization; and the poison metric is a train/test perturbation diagnostic. The raw split/provenance field must be read with the metric and target rather than interpreted as saying that every numeric value is a test loss.

For observation-level losses, the relevant rows are first aggregated within one scenario-replicate. Every other metric likewise contributes at most one value for that scenario-replicate-method-metric key. diagnostics.json then reports median, q10, and q90 across the four independent replicate-level values, together with ok, failure, and applicability rates for each scenario \(\times\) method \(\times\) metric group. Test rows, features, and EM iterations are not treated as independent replicates. The random-projection row uses one seed-derived basis per scenario-replicate, so its across-replicate quantiles reflect joint data-and-basis variation. Unlike targets are not normalized, averaged, ranked, or pooled.

The FA loading subspace and common covariance are conceptually distinct: the former ignores loading magnitudes, whereas \(\Lambda\Lambda^\top\) retains them while remaining invariant to orthogonal loading rotations. This first bounded implementation directly emits the loading-subspace angle, observable-covariance error, and uniqueness error, but does not emit a separate common-covariance norm. Therefore it makes no numerical common-covariance-magnitude recovery claim; “common structure” in the reported results refers only to the declared loading-subspace target.

Likewise, residual off-diagonal error and uniqueness relative error are not raw metrics in this benchmark. Selected floor, restart, and convergence state are row metadata that identify the frozen FA candidate; convergence state is also used to assign failure labels. Candidate train NLL may be retained in bounded selection diagnostics for verification, but neither train NLL nor a standalone convergence flag is emitted as a scored raw metric. The only scored FA selection/computation metrics are selected_validation_nll and optimizer_iterations.

Failure and applicability labels

Every expected scenario \(\times\) replicate \(\times\) method \(\times\) metric row is retained with exactly one status:

  • ok: the target applies and the value is finite and valid;
  • nonconvergence: the bounded optimizer or numerical procedure did not meet its declared convergence contract;
  • invalid: the computation, covariance, preprocessing, or value is invalid, including the deliberate pooled-preprocessing control where appropriate;
  • inapplicable: the method does not define that likelihood, target, or identified quantity under the scenario.

Every non-ok row has a machine-readable reason. Structural-identification inapplicability, model misspecification, finite recovery error, and optimizer nonconvergence use different reasons and are not substituted for one another. Rows do not disappear because a method failed or a target does not apply.

Interpretation limits

This benchmark can establish only simulator-, scenario-, preprocessing-, target-, metric-, and implementation-conditional behavior.

  • Close PCA recovery of the population principal subspace does not imply recovery of \(\operatorname{col}(\Lambda)\) when residual variances are heterogeneous.
  • Close FA loading-subspace recovery under applicable assumptions does not identify uniquely oriented loading columns or latent causes. Observable- covariance recovery is reported separately, and no common-covariance- magnitude recovery metric is emitted.
  • PPCA is restricted to isotropic residual variance; its principal-subspace relation does not make it equivalent to heterogeneous-noise FA.
  • Structural identification does not imply accurate finite recovery or optimizer convergence; convergence does not prove identification or correct specification.
  • Lower reconstruction error is not evidence of a better likelihood model, latent mechanism, or scientific representation.
  • Better held-out Gaussian NLL does not prove causal factors, robustness to contamination, or correctness outside the declared distribution.
  • High nuisance variance can make PCA scientifically unhelpful while still correct for its variance-defined target.
  • Neither rank two nor compact recovery proves model-free intrinsic dimension, a manifold, topology, dynamics, mechanism, biological representation, or real-data transfer.

There is no cross-target total score, universal winner, or authorization for Phase 5.

  likelihood, and posterior mean. It is not general diagonal-noise FA, and its
  likelihood does not retroactively make ordinary PCA probabilistic.
- **Bounded diagonal-noise FA:** a rank-two Gaussian model
  $C=\Lambda\Lambda^\top+\operatorname{diag}(\psi)$ fitted by deterministic
  bounded EM candidates and validation selection. Loading columns are compared
  only through rotation-invariant subspace/common-covariance targets.
- **True-parameter Gaussian reference:** uses known simulator parameters for
  likelihood and posterior reconstruction. It is an unavailable
  implementation/reference oracle, not a deployable fitted competitor. Under
  outlier contamination it is named and interpreted as a nominal clean
  Gaussian reference.

All lawful methods use the same frozen test observations. Population
eigenspaces, loadings, covariances, uniquenesses, and latent scores are scoring
truth or oracle inputs, never fitting information.

## Parameter selection

PCA and closed-form PPCA use the predeclared rank two and require no validation
selection. Fixed/random projections are frozen before data generation. For FA,
the training split fits every predeclared uniqueness-floor $\times$ restart
candidate; validation Gaussian NLL selects one candidate with a deterministic
tie rule. The selected floor and restart are frozen before test recovery and
scoring.

This finite candidate set is a bounded numerical procedure. It does not prove
global optimization, and validation selection cannot turn a misspecified
diagonal residual model into a correct one.

## Inference

For reconstruction methods, recovery receives frozen preprocessing and fitted
states plus test observations. PCA and projection baselines return Euclidean
reconstructions in original coordinates. PPCA and FA return model-conditional
Gaussian posterior-mean reconstructions. These are different estimators even
at the same rank.

No method receives test latent truth. The true-parameter Gaussian reference is
invoked through an explicitly oracle-labelled path. There is no temporal
information set: observations are independent static rows, and their ordering
has no scientific meaning.

## Scoring

Raw rows use metric-specific `target` metadata. The following task families
remain separate:

| Raw metric | Provenance, target, and interpretation |
|---|---|
| `heldout_reconstruction_mse` | Test observations and frozen reconstructions; original-coordinate observed reconstruction, including contaminated observations in `outliers`; not loading or covariance recovery |
| `latent_signal_reconstruction_mse` | Test clean common signal $\Lambda z$ retained by the generator, compared with the fitted reconstruction after subtracting the declared population mean; defined for every method that returns a finite test reconstruction, including mean, fixed/random projection, PCA, PPCA, selected FA, and the true-parameter reference. The two scaled-PCA controls are applicable only in `preprocessing_leakage`. In `outliers`, observations and fitted reconstructions include contamination while the target remains the uncontaminated $\Lambda z$, so the metric measures recovery of the clean simulated common contribution rather than reconstruction of the contaminated observation law |
| `heldout_gaussian_nll` | Test observations under a frozen PPCA, FA, or true-parameter Gaussian density; ordinary PCA and pure projections do not define this score. In `outliers`, this is a Gaussian score on contaminated observations, and the clean true-parameter model is only a nominal reference |
| `covariance_relative_frobenius` | Fitted model covariance versus population observable covariance; for `outliers`, the target is explicitly the nominal clean Gaussian covariance rather than the covariance of the contaminated law |
| `principal_subspace_angle_max_degrees` | Training-fitted PCA/PPCA space versus the population rank-two principal subspace of observable $C$; the value combines fitted training output with population scoring truth, not test observations |
| `loading_subspace_angle_max_degrees` | Training/validation-selected FA loading space versus $\operatorname{col}(\Lambda)$, or the exact reference against itself, only where the diagonal-residual and sufficient-rank identification conditions apply; population-truth scoring, not a test-split loss |
| `uniqueness_rmse` | Selected FA diagonal uniqueness versus population diagonal uniqueness only where the same identification conditions apply; the implementation emits RMSE, not uniqueness relative error |
| `selected_validation_nll` | Validation observations and the selected bounded FA candidate; a selection diagnostic, not held-out test performance |
| `optimizer_iterations` | Training optimization history of the selected FA candidate; a computational diagnostic. A run that reaches the bound is labelled `nonconvergence` |
| `identification_applicable` | Population scenario specification plus the sufficient rank diagnostic; a binary applicability record, not empirical recovery performance |
| `preprocessing_test_poison_parameter_change` | Difference between preprocessing/model parameters before and after deterministic test poisoning; uses training and test observations only for the declared leakage control and is not a candidate-method score |

Provenance is therefore metric-specific: only the held-out reconstruction,
clean-signal reconstruction, and Gaussian NLL metrics are direct test-row
aggregates. Subspace, covariance, uniqueness, and identification metrics join
fitted states to population truth; validation NLL comes from validation data;
optimizer iterations come from training optimization; and the poison metric is
a train/test perturbation diagnostic. The raw `split`/provenance field must be
read with the metric and target rather than interpreted as saying that every
numeric value is a test loss.

For observation-level losses, the relevant rows are first aggregated within
one scenario-replicate. Every other metric likewise contributes at most one
value for that scenario-replicate-method-metric key. `diagnostics.json` then
reports median, q10, and q90 across the four independent replicate-level
values, together with `ok`, failure, and applicability rates for each scenario
$\times$ method $\times$ metric group. Test rows, features, and EM iterations
are not treated as independent replicates. The random-projection row uses one
seed-derived basis per scenario-replicate, so its across-replicate quantiles
reflect joint data-and-basis variation. Unlike targets are not normalized,
averaged, ranked, or pooled.

The FA **loading subspace** and **common covariance** are conceptually distinct:
the former ignores loading magnitudes, whereas
$\Lambda\Lambda^\top$ retains them while remaining invariant to orthogonal
loading rotations. This first bounded implementation directly emits the
loading-subspace angle, observable-covariance error, and uniqueness error, but
does not emit a separate common-covariance norm. Therefore it makes no
numerical common-covariance-magnitude recovery claim; “common structure” in the
reported results refers only to the declared loading-subspace target.

Likewise, residual off-diagonal error and uniqueness relative error are not raw
metrics in this benchmark. Selected floor, restart, and convergence state are
row metadata that identify the frozen FA candidate; convergence state is also
used to assign failure labels. Candidate train NLL may be retained in bounded
selection diagnostics for verification, but neither train NLL nor a standalone
convergence flag is emitted as a scored raw metric. The only scored FA
selection/computation metrics are `selected_validation_nll` and
`optimizer_iterations`.

## Failure and applicability labels

Every expected scenario $\times$ replicate $\times$ method $\times$ metric row
is retained with exactly one status:

- `ok`: the target applies and the value is finite and valid;
- `nonconvergence`: the bounded optimizer or numerical procedure did not meet
  its declared convergence contract;
- `invalid`: the computation, covariance, preprocessing, or value is invalid,
  including the deliberate pooled-preprocessing control where appropriate;
- `inapplicable`: the method does not define that likelihood, target, or
  identified quantity under the scenario.

Every non-`ok` row has a machine-readable reason. Structural-identification
inapplicability, model misspecification, finite recovery error, and optimizer
nonconvergence use different reasons and are not substituted for one another.
Rows do not disappear because a method failed or a target does not apply.

## Interpretation limits

This benchmark can establish only simulator-, scenario-, preprocessing-,
target-, metric-, and implementation-conditional behavior.

- Close PCA recovery of the population principal subspace does not imply
  recovery of $\operatorname{col}(\Lambda)$ when residual variances are
  heterogeneous.
- Close FA loading-subspace recovery under applicable assumptions does not
  identify uniquely oriented loading columns or latent causes. Observable-
  covariance recovery is reported separately, and no common-covariance-
  magnitude recovery metric is emitted.
- PPCA is restricted to isotropic residual variance; its principal-subspace
  relation does not make it equivalent to heterogeneous-noise FA.
- Structural identification does not imply accurate finite recovery or
  optimizer convergence; convergence does not prove identification or correct
  specification.
- Lower reconstruction error is not evidence of a better likelihood model,
  latent mechanism, or scientific representation.
- Better held-out Gaussian NLL does not prove causal factors, robustness to
  contamination, or correctness outside the declared distribution.
- High nuisance variance can make PCA scientifically unhelpful while still
  correct for its variance-defined target.
- Neither rank two nor compact recovery proves model-free intrinsic dimension,
  a manifold, topology, dynamics, mechanism, biological representation, or
  real-data transfer.

There is no cross-target total score, universal winner, or authorization for
Phase 5.
